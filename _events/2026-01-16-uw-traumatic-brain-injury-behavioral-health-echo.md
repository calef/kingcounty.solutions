feed_content: "<article> <header> <h1>Welcome</h1> </header> <div> <div> <div> <h2>The
  Connection between Traumatic Brain Injury &amp; Mental Health</h2> <ul> <li>Traumatic
  brain injury (TBI) accounts for 2.8 million emergency department visits per year
  and contributes to a third of all injury-related deaths in the United States. </li>
  <li>As many as 1 in 3 patients continue to have impaired functioning 10 years after
  a TBI and nearly half develop a long-term disability.</li> <li>Many people who survive
  live with chronic TBI-related disabilities, in large part attributable to behavioral
  factors such as PTSD, depression, anxiety, irritability, impulsivity, anger/aggression,
  substance misuse, and cognitive impairment.</li> <li>TBI increases the risk and
  complexity of a host of behavioral health conditions and complicates treatments
  that require patient self-management.</li> <li>Persisting TBI symptoms can increase
  the risk of unemployment, caretaker burnout, and longer-term functional impairment
  or decline. </li> </ul> <p>Successful TBI recovery can depend in large part on access
  and adherence to behavioral health treatment.  Early identification and intervention
  improve outcomes, but community resources are scarce and fragmented. This program
  offers community providers who care for patients who have had a TBI with focused
  didactics and patient case consultations throughout Washington State.</p> </div>
  </div> <h2>Join us:</h2> <p><strong>WHAT: </strong>Connecting front-line providers
  with specialists to discuss evidence-based treatments and case consultation on the
  management of behavioral health problems in patients with TBI. </p> <p><strong>WHEN:
  </strong>Monthly online clinic held 1st and 3rd Fridays 12:00-1:30 PM PST</p> <p><strong>WHO</strong>:
  Any provider based in any setting who are managing patients with post-TBI behavioral
  health problems.</p> <p><strong>HOW: </strong>Join us via Zoom <strong>real-time,
  interactive video</strong> teleconference</p> <div> <div><a>Register Here</a></div>

The UW Traumatic Brain Injury - Behavioral Health ECHO program begins on January 16, 2026, and offers an opportunity for community providers to enhance their understanding of the connection between traumatic brain injury (TBI) and mental health. Attendees can expect focused discussions on evidence-based treatments and case consultations regarding behavioral health issues in TBI patients. The program aims to improve patient outcomes by facilitating early identification and intervention while addressing the challenges posed by behavioral factors such as PTSD, depression, and cognitive impairment.

This monthly online clinic will occur on the first and third Fridays from 12:00 to 1:30 PM PST, providing a platform for real-time, interactive learning. Open to all providers managing post-TBI behavioral health problems, this initiative is designed to bridge gaps in community resources and support effective treatment strategies.
