  Summary: </strong></p> <p>Obstructive sleep apnea is a serious and realtively common
  disorder that can have several interactions with dentistry. This presentation will
  be a succinct review of the evidence base on the topic. The relationship between
  OSA and craniofacial growth and development, as well as the current evidence for
  managing with dental therapies will be discussed.</p> <p><strong>Learning Objectives:</strong></p>
  <ol> <li>Describe the pathopshysiology of sleep-disordered breathing</li> <li>Define
  the relationship between dentofacial morphology and OSA</li> <li>Evaluate the evidence
  for maxillary expansion and mandibular advancement for OSA</li> </ol> <p><strong>Presenter
  Bio: </strong></p> <p>Dr. Benjamin Pliska is an associate professor of the University
  of British Columbia Faculty of Dentistry, an orthodontic consultant at BC Children’s
  Hospital, and maintains a private practice in Vancouver as a certified specialist
  in Orthodontics. He is a graduate of Western University School of Dentistry and
  obtained his Certificate in Orthodontics and Master’s Degree from the University
  of Minnesota. Dr. Pliska’s research interests include airway imaging and sleep medicine,
  topics on which he frequently lectures and publishes.</p> <p>Earn 1.5 continuing

Join us on March 5 at 5:30 p.m. for "Obstructive Sleep Apnea - an Update for the General Dentist." This informative course will provide dental professionals with a comprehensive review of the evidence surrounding obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and its implications for dental practice. Attendees can expect to learn about the pathophysiology of sleep-disordered breathing, the connection between dentofacial morphology and OSA, and evaluate current evidence for dental therapies such as maxillary expansion and mandibular advancement.

The session will be led by Dr. Benjamin Pliska, an associate professor at the University of British Columbia's Faculty of Dentistry and a certified orthodontic specialist. Participants will earn 1.5 continuing dental education credits. Don't miss this opportunity to enhance your understanding of OSA and its management in dentistry.
