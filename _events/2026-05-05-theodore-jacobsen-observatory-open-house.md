  </ul> <h2> Welcome</h2> <p>Welcome to the web page of the public events program
  at the Theodor Jacobsen Observatory (TJO) of the University of Washington.  Come
  and join us for open houses where we offer a view through our 1895 telescope on
  clear dark nights. In addition, enjoy evening talks and other activities during
  your visit for people of all ages, from 6 to 100. Tickets are free.</p> <p>Programs
  normally take place at the observatory on the first and third Tuesday evenings of
  April through September, rain or shine.  The staff are entirely volunteers, including
  undergraduates from the “League of Astronomers” within the Astronomy Department
  as well as members of the Seattle Astronomical Society (under the auspices of the
  UW Astronomy Department).</p> <h2> TJO Evening Public Events</h2> <p><span>Sky viewing
  is the main event of the evening.  However, nightfall comes late. So doors open
  at 8 or 9 PM from April through September (see below for dates and times) while
  the sky is still light.  The first major event of the evening is a public talk by
  students or faculty.  Then, once skies are clear and dark, our spirited volunteers
  from the </span><a><span>Seattle Astronomical Society</span></a><span> open the
  old dome, point the telescope to objects of interest, and public viewing begins
  once the skies darken unless the skies are overcast.</span></p> <p><span>The lecture
  and other events in the program </span>are always taking place until the observatory
  closes for the evening<span> — no matter what the weather</span>.  You will enjoy
  the 1895 “transit room” with ornate 19<sup>th</sup> century brass equipment that
  was used to calibrate the time on precise clocks used throughout the state.  (Time
  is now kept with atomic clocks at the U.S Naval Observatory.)  Our volunteers also
  have live exhibits that appeal to everyone.</p> <p>The TJO honors the remarkable
  career of Prof. Theodor Jacobsen at UW between 1928 until his death at age 102. 
  (His last book was published in 1999.)  Built in 1895, the Observatory and its lovely
  brass 6-inch refracting telescope are presently committed to public viewing and
  exploration of the wonders of the Universe.  The telescope was designed in the days
  before electricity was common. So, the telescope tracks the sky using its original
  gear-and-weight mechanism—much like the wind-up drive of a grandfather clock.  It
  was used by UW astronomers for research until about 1945.</p> <p><em>Note: there
  is an ADA ramp to the classroom inside the observatory; however, access to the dome
  is possible only via a narrow wooden stairway built in the 1890s. We urge you to
  dress warmly!  </em></p> <p><em><strong>A special thanks to members of the </strong></em><a><em><strong>Seattle
  Astronomical Society</strong></em></a><em><strong> for their many years of participation
  at the TJO and dedication to public outreach!  Please see their web pages for their
  other outreach events.</strong></em></p> <h2> Evening Event Schedule</h2> <p><strong>Public

  <h2> Open-House Tickets and Reservations</h2> <p>Tickets are free but absolutely
  necessary since space is limited (the TJO classroom holds only 45 people and the
  dome access is limited to 12 people at a time.)  You will be assigned into groups
  for access to the telescope as you pick up your tickets when doors open, first come
  and first served.   Your tickets will be handed to you as you enter the observatory
  classroom (far left door).  We advise groups with young children to arrive promptly.</p>
  <p>Please send your ticket request to <a>tjores@uw.edu</a>.  Be sure to include
  the date you would like to attend, the name for your reservation, and how many people
  will be joining you. <span>We apologize that we may not be able to accommodate every
  request for tickets.  If you are unable to get a ticket for one night then feel
  welcome to submit another request for a later date.  </span><em><span>We may not
  be able to host groups of 12 or more visitors at open houses.</span></em></p> <h2>
  Group Events</h2> <p>We are especially glad to welcome adult recreation groups,
  groups of students with special needs, K-12 classes, and home-schooling groups (and
  more) to the Observatory.  We can work with the supervisors/leaders/teachers on
  topics that they would like presented. We also offer a wide variety of activities
  that we could include in the evening’s program.</p> <p>Please make all requests
  at <a>tjores@uw.edu</a>.  Be sure to include your name and email, the nature of
  your group, how many people will be coming (limit 45), several dates that will work
  for you, and let us know if  special arrangements are needed. <em>Please note: the
  number of special group visits is limited.  Schedules and dates depend on volunteer
  availability.</em></p> <p><strong>Talks at our planetarium</strong> are also available
  to school groups on Friday afternoons at no charge.  Please see <a>UW Planetarium
  Page</a> for more information and to make reservations.</p> <h2> Directions &amp;
  Parking</h2> <p>The observatory is located about 100 yards south of the north campus
  entrance at NE 45th and 17<sup>th</sup> NE.  Try entering “Theodor Jacobsen Observatory”
  or GPS coordinates “47.660503, -122.309424” into your favorite map program.  The
  TJO is a bit difficult to see from the entrance road, Memorial Drive, since it lies
  is in a clump of trees immediately east of the parking kiosks.  The Burke Museum
  is nearby.</p> <p>Pay parking is available in the adjoining lot “N5” for $6.50 per
  car (the rate can change). Spaces are usually wide open near the observatory each
  evening.  See <a>Directions &amp; Parking page</a> for prepaid permits or pay using
  <em>PayByPhone </em>on your phone after you arrive. As a last resort you can also
  get parking passes for lot N5 at the parking kiosk at the entrance to the UW Underground
  Parking Garage east of NE 40<sup>th</sup> and 15<sup>th</sup> NE.</p> <p>Alternately,
  use light rail to the U District U-Link station (Brooklyn and 45<sup>th</sup>) and
  walk three blocks east on 45<sup>th</sup> to the TJO. During UW holidays you might
  find parking on an adjoining street (where many UW students park their cars).</p>

Join the Theodor Jacobsen Observatory for an open house on May 5, 2026, from 9 p.m. to 11 p.m. Located at the University of Washington, the event offers attendees the chance to view celestial wonders through the historic 1895 telescope, weather permitting. The evening will feature engaging public talks by students or faculty, followed by telescope viewing once the sky darkens. This family-friendly event is suitable for all ages, and tickets are free but required due to limited space. Visitors can explore the observatory's charming transit room and enjoy live exhibits. The observatory is open rain or shine, and volunteers from the Seattle Astronomical Society will assist throughout the evening. Dress warmly and prepare for a night of astronomy and discovery at this unique venue.
