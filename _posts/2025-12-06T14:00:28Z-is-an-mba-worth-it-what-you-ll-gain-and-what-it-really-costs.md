The Master of Business Administration (MBA) remains a popular graduate degree, with nearly 25% of master's degrees awarded in business from 2021 to 2022. The value of an MBA varies based on individual circumstances and career goals. While many graduates experience a significant return on investment (ROI), concerns about rising tuition and student debt—averaging over $60,000—are prompting potential students to reassess the degree's worth.

Despite challenges posed by technological advancements like artificial intelligence, the demand for MBAs persists, particularly in leadership roles across various industries. Graduates typically see salary increases averaging 33% post-degree, with median starting salaries around $125,000. An MBA can enhance career advancement, earning potential, and provide networking opportunities.

However, it may not be the best fit for everyone, especially early-career professionals or those seeking less expensive alternatives. Programs like Master of Science in Management and Leadership (MSML) or Master in Management (MiM) can better suit less experienced candidates. Ultimately, prospective students should weigh their personal and professional goals, financing, and the program format—full-time or part-time—before committing to an MBA.
