  <p>On Saturday, Dec. 13, 2025, the Douglass-Truth Branch was jamming. Neighbors, families, community leaders, elders, and library staff filled every corner of the branch at 2300 E. Yesler Way to <a>celebrate the 50th anniversary</a> of its community-chosen name.</p>



  <p>Almost exactly a half-century earlier, on Dec. 5, 1975, the branch was renamed in honor of abolitionists Sojourner Truth and Frederick Douglass, a process that took <a>years of community advocacy</a>.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Branch-with-balloons-sm.jpg?resize=983%2C633&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>The Douglass-Truth was decked out on Saturday, Dec. 13</figcaption></figure>



  <p>The day started with a branch history tour by children’s librarian Mynique Adams, who highlighted the importance of community and organizations such as Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, Inc.®, Delta Upsilon Omega Chapter (AKA-DUO) coming together to save the library then named the Yesler branch. </p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Community-photo-2-correct.jpg?resize=1024%2C696&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>The day started off with a branch history tour. Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>Together, they created a collection that celebrated and affirmed Black children, and renamed the branch to better reflect the power, pride, and beauty of the Black community. </p>



  <p></p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Branch-history-tour-2025-SPL-Douglass-Truth-LCarmona-8405-sm.jpg?resize=1000%2C666&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>The tour visited the African-American Collection on the lower level. Photo by Leo Carmona</figcaption></figure>



  <p>Visitors on the tour contributed their own stories and reflections, including a very special guest. Retired librarian Audrey Wright worked at the branch in the 1960s and early 1970s, alongside librarian Jim Welsh, partnering with AKA-DUO and the Black Friends of the Yesler Library to help build the African American Collection and revitalize the branch. </p>



  <p>It was an honor that Ms. Wright and her sister, who also worked at the branch, took the time to attend the celebration and share their knowledge and memories. </p>



  <p>The tour also visited some of the branch’s artworks, including the <a>“Home of Good: A Black Seattle Storyquilt,”</a> which was donated to the collection by artist Storme Webber.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mynique-showing-quilt-correct.jpg?resize=1001%2C748&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Artist Storme Webber’s magnificent quilt is a loving testimony to the history and community of the Central District. Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-SPL-Douglass-Truth-LCarmona-8299-sm.jpg?resize=1000%2C714&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Visitors browse artwork by Charles White and Elizabeth Catlett.</figcaption></figure>



  <p>Opportunities to make art were also on hand. Crafters, artists and dreamers of all ages gathered to do creative journaling and crafts with local artist Danitra Hunter.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/DTH-renaming-crafts-LCarmona-8828-sm-1.jpg?resize=1000%2C666&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>A Jazz quartet from Garfield High School paid homage in musical form to the longstanding partnership between Garfield High School and the branch.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/DTH-renaming-Garfield-jazz-band-LCarmona-8918-sm.jpg?resize=1024%2C732&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>People mingled, made art, made friends, and talked about the branch’s past, present and future.   </p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Community-photo-correct.jpg?resize=1024%2C723&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>The afternoon ended with a lively conversation about the branch’s African American Collection by Special Collections managing librarian Sean Lanksbury and African-American Collection curator Brian Den Hartog-Lindsey. </p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Community-4.jpg?resize=1024%2C693&amp;ssl=1"></a></figure>



  <p>AKA-DUO just celebrated its 61st year of supporting the collection.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/AKA-BHS-SPL-group-photo-DTH-renaming-LCarmona-9114-sm.jpg?resize=1000%2C666&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>AKA-DUO and the Black Heritage Society leaders gather with Special Collections managing librarian Sean Lanksbury and African-American Collection Curator Brian Den Hartog-Lindsey. Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>The audience, including leaders with AKA-DUO and the Black Heritage Society of Washington State, emphasized the collection’s importance as a source of community research, pride and inspiration.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2025-SPL-Douglass-Truth-LCarmona-9021.jpg?resize=1000%2C666&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Brian and Sean led a lively conversation about the collection. Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>Brian shared plans for preserving and expanding the collection, and revealed an exciting new addition: a first edition of a compilation titled “Slave Songs,” originally published in 1867.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Brian-Den-Hartog-Lindsey-with-Slave-Songs-sm-1.jpg?resize=978%2C1024&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>“We want to re-engage the community with the collection and have it do what it was meant to do: Bring people in,” said Brian. </p>



  <p>Many thanks to AKA-DUO and the Black Heritage Society of Washington State for their longstanding partnership and support. We are also grateful to The Seattle Public Library Foundation for sponsoring the event. </p>



  <p>We also want to collectively recognize our Douglass-Truth staff members, who worked tirelessly for months to create a joyous and community-filled celebration that honored this branch’s resilient history, deep roots, and partnerships. </p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/DTH-staff.jpg?resize=1000%2C714&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Douglass-Truth Branch staff working the circulation desk during the celebration, with Eddie Walker’s portrait of Sojourner Truth above. Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</figcaption></figure>



  <p>We invite everyone to stop by the branch to explore the African-American Collection, enjoy the branch’s artwork, soak up its history, or to simply browse.</p>



  <p><em><strong>Caption for top photo: </strong>From left to right, Black Heritage Society president Stephanie-Johnson-Toliver, Valerie Buford</em> and <em>Dolores Walker</em>, <em>all proud C.D. residents and library users, show off 60th anniversary tote bags. Eddie Walker’s “Sojourner Truth” painting rises in the background. Photo by Leo Carmona, The Seattle Public Library</em></p>

On Dec. 13, 2025, the Douglass-Truth Branch celebrated its 50th anniversary since being renamed in honor of abolitionists Sojourner Truth and Frederick Douglass. The event drew community members, leaders, and library staff to the branch at 2300 E. Yesler Way. The day began with a history tour led by children’s librarian Mynique Adams, who highlighted the significance of community efforts in renaming the library, originally the Yesler branch. Visitors shared stories and reflections, including retired librarian Audrey Wright, who helped build the branch’s African American Collection in the 1960s and 1970s. The celebration featured a jazz quartet from Garfield High School, honoring the school's partnership with the library. Attendees engaged in creative activities and discussions about the African American Collection, led by Special Collections librarian Sean Lanksbury and curator Brian Den Hartog-Lindsey. They emphasized the collection's role in community pride and research, unveiling plans for its preservation and expansion, including the addition of a first edition of "Slave Songs." The event recognized the support of the Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority, Inc.® and the Black Heritage Society of Washington State, as well as the efforts of the Douglass-Truth staff in organizing the celebration.
