  class=\"field field--name-body field--type-text-with-summary field--label-hidden
  field--item\"><h2>Full northbound closure of I-5 over Ship Canal Bridge planned
  for Jan. 9-12 weekend</h2><p>SEATTLE – The largest active highway preservation effort
  in Washington state will enter its second year of construction beginning beginning
  the weekend of Jan. 9-12, weather permitting. This temporary full closure of northbound
  Interstate 5 in Seattle will allow crews to establish a work zone to perform important
  work on the Ship Canal Bridge. During the construction lane closures, the I-5 express
  lanes will only run northbound 24 hours a day.</p><p>Once the interstate reopens,
  travelers can expect months of partial lane closures as crews working for the Washington
  State Department of Transportation work to preserve and extend the life of the bridge.
  Due to the time-intensive nature of removing the old roadway surface, completing
  structural repairs and placing new concrete, crews must establish a long-term work
  zone across the bridge. The work zone also helps safeguard the crews undertaking
  this work.</p><p>Leaders from WSDOT, Seattle Department of Transportation, Sound
  Transit and King County Metro emphasized their shared commitment on Tuesday, Jan.
  6, to help keep people and goods moving safely and reliably through the region during
  construction.</p><p>“Preserving the I-5 Ship Canal Bridge is essential to a safe
  and reliable regional transportation system,” said Secretary of Transportation Julie
  Meredith. “Building on our success from 2025, we know we can manage the traffic
  impacts of four weeks of lane reductions with thoughtful planning. We’re asking
  everyone to consider choosing alternate routes, carpooling, adjusting travel times
  or using transit to help keep everyone moving.”</p><p>The&nbsp;<a href=\"/construction-planning/search-projects/revive-i-5-ship-canal-bridge-preservation\">Revive
  I-5: Ship Canal Bridge Preservation project</a> is a multi-year effort that includes
  repairing and resurfacing the upper bridge deck, replacing aged expansion joints
  and improving drainage. In 2025, WSDOT started this project by completing early
  drainage improvements and repairs. In 2026 construction will focus on the northbound
  bridge deck and in 2027 the southbound bridge deck. The project is expected to be
  complete by the end of 2027.</p><p>During this work, close coordination between
  state, city and transit agencies helps minimize regional impacts and provide travelers
  with reliable alternatives.</p><p>Angela Brady, interim director, Seattle Department
  of Transportation shared, “SDOT is here to help you navigate the city during Revive
  I-5 construction, so you can continue to enjoy everything Seattle has to offer.
  We encourage everyone to plan ahead, stay informed, and take transit. SDOT monitors
  the city’s transportation network 24/7, helping keep buses frequent and reliable
  while also maintaining emergency access. We’ll continue to collaborate with regional
  partners, manage the public right of way, and communicate with the public to help
  you get around.”</p><p>“Whether it’s a trip to work or a Seahawks playoff game,
  Sound Transit and our partners are working closely to get you where you need to

A full northbound closure of Interstate 5 over the Ship Canal Bridge in Seattle is scheduled for the weekend of Jan. 9-12, weather permitting. This closure marks the start of the second year of the Revive I-5: Ship Canal Bridge Preservation project, aimed at extending the bridge's life through repairs and resurfacing. During the closure, crews will establish a work zone in the two left lanes of the bridge.

Once reopened on Jan. 12, northbound I-5 will experience two-lane reductions until early June, with a pause during the FIFA World Cup from June 8 to July 10, when all lanes will be open. Following the World Cup, work will resume, resulting in further lane reductions through the end of 2026.

The Washington State Department of Transportation, along with local transit agencies, is urging travelers to consider alternate routes, carpooling, or using public transit to minimize traffic disruptions. Travelers can access real-time information through the WSDOT mobile app or website and are advised to plan ahead for potential delays. The project is expected to be completed by the end of 2027.
