In the 100th episode of the Peace Meal podcast, clinicians Mem Wood and Elysse Thebner Miller from The Emily Program discuss Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder (ARFID), emphasizing its distinctiveness from typical picky eating. They explain that ARFID can develop at any age and significantly impact nutrition, growth, and daily life. The hosts outline the three subtypes of ARFID and describe the treatment process, which includes renourishment, exposure-based food discovery, and body-awareness skills. They highlight signs that indicate when to seek help, noting that individuals often report a limited range of foods they consume, which can shrink their social and functional world.

The episode also introduces The Emily Program's new specialized residential and inpatient treatment for adults with ARFID in the Raleigh–Durham area, the first of its kind in the U.S. Wood and Miller stress the importance of addressing ARFID concerns early and provide guidance for supporting loved ones in seeking help. They encourage listeners to reach out for resources and support if they suspect they or someone they know may be struggling with ARFID.
