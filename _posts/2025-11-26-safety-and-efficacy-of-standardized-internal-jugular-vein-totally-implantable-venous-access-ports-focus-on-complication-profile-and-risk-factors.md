A study evaluated the safety and effectiveness of ultrasound-guided implantation of totally implantable venous access ports (TIVAPs) in cancer patients, focusing on complications and risk factors. Researchers analyzed data from 175 patients who received right internal jugular vein (IJV) TIVAPs between September 2020 and March 2024. The standardized protocol included ultrasound-guided puncture and postoperative chest X-ray, achieving a 100% procedural success rate. The average operative time was 40 minutes, with no early complications observed. However, 14 patients (8.0%) developed late complications, primarily skin ulcers around the port. A multivariable analysis identified concurrent diabetes mellitus and hypertension as the only significant independent risk factors for complications. Age, sex, tumor type, and body mass index were not significant. The findings suggest that ultrasound-guided IJV TIVAP implantation is a safe and effective procedure, with the presence of diabetes and hypertension highlighting the importance of preoperative risk assessment and management in these patients.
