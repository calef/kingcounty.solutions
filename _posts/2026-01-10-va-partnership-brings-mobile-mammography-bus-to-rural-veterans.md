  <h2>On the road to prevention</h2>



  <p>The mobile mammography bus is back at VA Tennessee Valley Healthcare System (TVHS), continuing its mission to make breast cancer screenings easier and more accessible for Veterans.<br><br>The mobile service, offered in partnership with CHI Memorial MaryEllen Locher Breast Center, provides on-site mammograms to eligible Veterans, eliminating the need to travel to off-site locations for routine breast cancer screenings. After five rounds of bus visits this year, the program will conclude the 2025 schedule during Breast Cancer Awareness Month as the TVHS Women’s Health team begins planning for 2026.<br><br>“We’ve definitely had more opportunities this year than last,” said Melissa McNabb, a women’s health registered nurse at the Chattanooga VA Clinic who helps coordinate the mobile mammography events. “We’ve been more proactive about scheduling, and our Veterans have really responded.”</p>



  <h2>Using modern tools to deliver timely care</h2>


  <div>
  <figure><a><img src="https://news.va.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/01/Mobile-Mammography-Bus-2025-TVHS_r1-scaled.jpg?w=730"></a><figcaption>The CHI Memorial MaryEllen Locher Breast Center’s mobile mammography bus conveniently parks outside of the Chattanooga VA Clinic and can service up to 30 Veterans per visit.</figcaption></figure>
  </div>


  <p>Under the <a>SERVICE Act</a> and <a>PACT Act</a>, eligibility now includes Veterans who may have been exposed to toxic substances during service, further widening access to these life-saving screenings. Eligibility for VA mammography screenings typically began at age 40 for those enrolled in VA health care.<br><br>Each visit, the mobile unit typically serves nearly 30 Veterans, offering quick and convenient screenings. McNabb attributes the increase in participation to improved outreach and automated text alerts sent through <a>VA’s Annie App</a>, a system notifying Veterans of self-care reminders and available health care services. “It’s been instrumental,” McNabb said. “They get a text saying the bus is here and can respond right from their phone to schedule.”</p>



  <h2>Coordinated care after screening</h2>



  <p>At TVHS, every screening result is reviewed by a breast cancer care coordinator who ensures Veterans receive timely and seamless follow-up care. The specialized clinician reviews each mammography report, assists with diagnostic scheduling and connects Veterans to additional breast health services as needed.<br><br>According to Lynn Daugherty, a registered nurse and the women’s health program manager for TVHS, this personalized approach ensures no Veteran falls through the cracks.<br><br>“Our breast cancer coordinator tracks every mammogram result, makes sure follow-up appointments are scheduled, and stays with Veterans through every stage of the process,” Daugherty said. “Our Veterans know there’s someone watching out for them every step of the way.”</p>



  <h2>Reaching rural Veterans and building for the future</h2>



  <p>The mobile mammogram bus has proven especially beneficial for Veterans in North Georgia and rural areas, allowing them to access care closer to home rather than traveling to larger facilities in Nashville or Murfreesboro. The Women’s Health team understands that making care easy to access is key to preventive health for Veterans, not just reactionary treatment.</p>



  <p>“If you make it easy for Veterans to do preventive health care, they will do it,” McNabb said. “You can tell people all day that early detection can save their life, but convenience makes all the difference.”<br><br>To schedule a mammogram through the mobile bus at the Chattanooga VA Clinic, can contact your primary care team, send a secure message through <a>My HealtheVet</a>, or respond to an Annie App text alert when the service becomes available. To sign up for Annie text alerts, ask for enrollment through your primary care team.<br></p>

The mobile mammography bus has returned to the VA Tennessee Valley Healthcare System (TVHS), making breast cancer screenings more accessible for Veterans. In partnership with CHI Memorial MaryEllen Locher Breast Center, the bus provides on-site mammograms, eliminating the need for travel to off-site locations. This year, the program has offered five rounds of visits, concluding its schedule during Breast Cancer Awareness Month. Melissa McNabb, a women’s health registered nurse at the Chattanooga VA Clinic, noted an increase in participation due to improved scheduling and outreach efforts, including automated text alerts through the VA’s Annie App.

The mobile unit can service up to 30 Veterans per visit and now includes those exposed to toxic substances during service. Each screening result is reviewed by a breast cancer care coordinator who ensures timely follow-up care. This personalized approach aims to prevent Veterans from missing critical health services. The mobile mammography bus is particularly beneficial for Veterans in rural areas, allowing easier access to care. McNabb emphasized that convenience is crucial for encouraging preventive health measures, stating that when care is easy to access, Veterans are more likely to participate.
