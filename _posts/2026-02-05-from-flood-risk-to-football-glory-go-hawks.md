feed_content: "<p>By Steven Schauer, Deputy Executive Director</p>\n\n\n\n<p>The King
  County Flood Control District is riding the same wave of excitement as the rest
  of the region after watching the Seattle Seahawks punch their ticket to the Super
  Bowl by winning the NFC Championship. What a game! Strategy, teamwork, resilience,
  and a whole lot of heart, qualities we admire both on the football field <em>and</em>
  in flood risk reduction.</p>\n\n\n\n<p>So, as the Seahawks get ready for the biggest
  game of the year, we couldn’t resist having a little fun by drawing some comparisons
  between football terms and <a>flood terms</a>.  And just like the Seahawks are working
  hard every day to win the Super Bowl by strengthening their preparation, fundamentals,
  and accountability across the team, we’re working hard every day to win flood risk
  reduction by helping protect people, property, and communities across King County.</p>\n\n\n<div>\n<figure><img
  src=\"https://kingcountyfloodcontrol.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Seattle-Seahawks-fans-at-Lumen-Field-credtit-Seattle-Seahawks-seahawks.com_-1024x557.jpg\"><figcaption>Seattle
  Seahawks fans at Lumen Field: Credit: Seattle Seahawks, seahawks.com</figcaption></figure>\n</div>\n\n\n<h2><strong><img
  src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f3df.png\"> The Basin: Where
  the Game Is Played</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>In flood risk management, a <strong>basin</strong>
  (sometimes called a <strong>watershed</strong>) is the land area where rain and
  snowmelt collect and drain to a common outlet, such as a river, lake, or ocean.
  Think of it as the <strong>football field</strong> itself. Everything that happens,
  rainfall, runoff, and river flow, unfolds within the basin, just like every play
  unfolds on the field. Understanding the full field is essential if you want to win
  the game.</p>\n\n\n<div>\n<figure><img src=\"https://kingcountyfloodcontrol.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2301_12859w_KC_watersheds_web_1440-1024x715.jpg\"><figcaption>King
  County Watersheds Map</figcaption></figure>\n</div>\n\n\n<h2><strong><img src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f9e0.png\">
  The Game Plan: Capital Improvement Programs</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>Every championship
  team has a playbook. For us, that’s our <strong>Capital Improvement Program (CIP)</strong>,
  a long-term plan that prioritizes flood risk reduction projects across the county.
  Like a smart game plan, a strong CIP helps us anticipate challenges, invest wisely,
  and move the ball steadily down the field toward safer, more resilient communities.</p>\n\n\n\n<h2><strong><img
  src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f6e1.png\"> Defense Wins Championships
  (and Reduces Flood Risk)</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>In football, a solid defense keeps
  the other team from breaking through. In flood risk reduction, <strong>flood protection
  facilities</strong>, like <strong>levees</strong>, <strong>floodwalls</strong>,
  and <strong>pump stations</strong>, play the same role. They’re our defensive line,
  helping to reduce flood impacts and protect communities when rivers rise and conditions
  get tough.</p>\n\n\n\n<figure>\n<figure><img src=\"https://kingcountyfloodcontrol.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Seahawks-Defense-credit-Seattle-Seahawks-seahawks.com_-1024x561.jpg\"><figcaption>Seattle
  Seahawks defense. Credit: Seattle Seahawks, seahawks.com</figcaption></figure>\n\n\n\n<figure><img
  src=\"https://kingcountyfloodcontrol.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/20250716_143328-1024x768.jpg\"><figcaption>Black
  River Pump Station</figcaption></figure>\n</figure>\n\n\n\n<h2><strong><img src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/26a1.png\">
  Flash Flooding: The Defensive Blitz</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p><strong>Flash flooding</strong>
  can happen fast, with little warning, often after intense rainfall. It’s the flood
  equivalent of a <strong>defensive blitz</strong>: sudden, aggressive, and capable
  of catching everyone off guard if you’re not prepared. That’s why forecasting, monitoring,
  and preparation are such critical parts of flood risk management.</p>\n\n\n\n<h2><strong><img
  src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f4cf.png\"> Reading the Field:
  River Gages</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>Quarterbacks read the defense before the snap.
  Flood managers read <strong>river gages</strong>, which tell us how high water levels
  are rising in real time. Those measurements help inform decisions, just like reading
  coverage helps a QB decide whether to pass, hand off, or scramble.</p>\n\n\n\n<h2><strong><img
  src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f6a8.png\"> Flood Alerts &amp;
  Flood Warnings: Calling an Audible at the Line</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>Speaking
  of quarterbacks, great quarterbacks don’t just stick to the original play as they
  <strong>read the defense at the line of scrimmage</strong>. When they spot danger,
  they call an <strong>audible</strong>, adjusting the play to protect the team and
  avoid a big loss.</p>\n\n\n\n<p>That’s exactly how <strong>flood alerts and flood
  warnings</strong> work.</p>\n\n\n\n<p>A <strong>flood alert</strong> is like a quarterback
  noticing the defense starting to shift. Conditions are changing, and it’s time to
  pay attention, get ready, and be prepared to act. A <strong>flood warning</strong>,
  on the other hand, is the audible being called, danger is imminent or already happening,
  and it’s time to move quickly and decisively to stay safe.</p>\n\n\n\n<p>Just like
  a well-timed audible can save a play, early flood alerts and flood warnings help
  people, communities, and emergency responders prepare, adjust, and respond before
  floodwaters cause serious harm. Awareness, communication, and quick action can make
  all the difference between a safe outcome and a costly turnover.</p>\n\n\n\n<h2><strong><img
  src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f300.png\"> Channel Migration:
  Jumping Offside</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>Rivers are dynamic, and sometimes they shift
  course over time, a process known as <strong>channel migration</strong>. In football
  terms, that’s like a defender jumping <strong>offside</strong>. It’s an unexpected
  movement that changes the play and requires a quick response to avoid bigger consequences
  down the line.</p>\n\n\n<div>\n<figure><img src=\"https://kingcountyfloodcontrol.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/yellow-flag-on-the-play-credit-ESPN-espn.com_.jpg\"><figcaption>Yellow
  flag on the play. Credit: ESPN, espn.com</figcaption></figure>\n</div>\n\n\n<h2><strong><img
  src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f6ab.png\"> Avoiding a Breach</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>In
  flood terms, a <strong>breach</strong> is something we work hard to prevent, it’s
  when water breaks through a levee, floodwall, or some other flood risk reduction
  infrastructure. In football, it’s the equivalent of a blown coverage, costly penalty,
  or turnover at the worst possible moment. Preparation, maintenance, and teamwork
  help keep everyone protected and the game under control.</p>\n\n\n\n<h2><strong><img
  src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f389.png\"> Cheering as the
  12s — On Game Day and Rainy Days</strong></h2>\n\n\n\n<p>As the Seahawks head to
  the <strong>Super Bowl</strong>, the King County Flood Control District will proudly
  be cheering right alongside the rest of the <strong>12s</strong>. And just like
  the team prepares all season for a championship moment, we know that <strong>preparation
  is what helps everyone perform at their best when it really counts</strong>, on
  the field <em>and</em> at home.</p>\n\n\n<div>\n<figure><img src=\"https://kingcountyfloodcontrol.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/12-flag-credit-seattle-seahawks-seahawks.com_.jpg\"><figcaption>12
  Flag on top of Seattle Space Needle. Credit: Seattle Seahawks, seahawks.com</figcaption></figure>\n</div>\n\n\n<p>Think
  about how you get ready for a Super Bowl party. You don’t wait until kickoff to
  start planning. You stock up on snacks (and beverages), check the TV setup, invite
  friends, and make sure you’re ready for anything from overtime to unexpected guests.
  Flood preparedness works the same way.</p>\n\n\n\n<p>Taking a few simple steps <em>before</em>
  high water shows up, so you’re not scrambling when conditions change, is what it
  means to <strong>Be Flood Ready</strong>. That includes knowing your flood risk,
  signing up for flood alerts and flood warnings, understanding evacuation routes,
  and having a plan for your household, pets, and valuables. Just like a great host
  anticipates what guests might need, flood preparedness helps residents anticipate
  potential risks and respond with confidence.</p>\n\n\n\n<p>Our <a>Flood Resources
  webpage</a> is your go-to playbook. It’s where residents can find practical tools,
  guidance, and information to help prepare for flooding, much like a checklist for
  throwing a successful Super Bowl party. A little preparation up front can make a
  big difference when the pressure is on!</p>\n\n\n\n<p>So, as we fire up the grills,
  put on our jerseys, and cheer the Seahawks on to victory, let’s also remember that
  flood preparedness is a team sport.</p>\n\n\n\n<p><strong>We’re all stronger, and
  safer, when we’re ready.</strong></p>\n\n\n\n<p>From basins to blitzes, flood alerts
  to audibles, and end zones to flood zones, the King County Flood Control District
  is proud to stand with the Seahawks and the <strong>12s</strong>.</p>\n\n\n\n<h2><strong>Go
  Hawks—bring it home! <img src=\"https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f499.png\"><img
