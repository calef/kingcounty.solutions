  <p>Our priority during active flood events is clear communication, public safety, and supporting coordination efforts across jurisdictions as communities respond and recover.</p>



  <h2><strong>Understanding Flood Phases in King County</strong></h2>



  <p>King County uses flood phases to reflect river flood conditions. The higher the phase, the more water flows in our rivers, and the greater the likelihood of flooding. These phases are based on historical flood conditions unique to each river basin, helping emergency managers, public agencies, and residents understand the severity of an event.</p>



  <h3>Flood Phases Explained</h3>



  <ul>
  <li>Flood Phase 0 – No flooding expected<br>Rivers are within normal ranges.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 1 – Be on alert<br>Conditions are being closely monitored, and preparations begin for a possible flood event.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 2 – Minor flooding expected<br>Low-lying areas may flood, and impacts begin to occur.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 3 – Moderate flooding expected<br>Floodwaters may begin to affect homes, roads, and infrastructure.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 4 – Severe flooding expected<br>Significant flooding with potentially threatening impacts to life, property, and critical infrastructure.</li>
  </ul>



  <p>During the current event, multiple river basins across King County have reached high flood phases, underscoring the seriousness of this situation.</p>

  <h2><strong>Understanding Flood Phases in King County</strong></h2>



  <p>King County uses flood phases to reflect river flood conditions. The higher the phase, the more water flows in our rivers, and the greater the likelihood of flooding. These phases are based on historical flood conditions unique to each river basin, helping emergency managers, public agencies, and residents understand the severity of an event.</p>



  <h3>Flood Phases Explained</h3>



  <ul>
  <li>Flood Phase 0 – No flooding expected<br>Rivers are within normal ranges.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 1 – Be on alert<br>Conditions are being closely monitored, and preparations begin for a possible flood event.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 2 – Minor flooding expected<br>Low-lying areas may flood, and impacts begin to occur.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 3 – Moderate flooding expected<br>Floodwaters may begin to affect homes, roads, and infrastructure.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 4 – Severe flooding expected<br>Significant flooding with potentially threatening impacts to life, property, and critical infrastructure.</li>
  </ul>



  <p>During the current event, multiple river basins across King County have reached high flood phases, underscoring the seriousness of this situation.</p>

King County is currently experiencing widespread flooding, prompting a shift in focus from a planned Year-End Review to an explanation of flood phases and their significance. The county defines flood phases to indicate river conditions, with higher phases representing increased water flow and a greater likelihood of flooding. Flood Phase 0 indicates no expected flooding, while Phase 1 calls for alertness and preparation. Phase 2 suggests minor flooding in low-lying areas, and Phase 3 indicates moderate flooding that may affect homes and infrastructure. Phase 4 signifies severe flooding with potential threats to life and property.

During the current flood event, multiple river basins have reached high flood phases, emphasizing the seriousness of the situation. The article provides comparisons of river conditions during normal and flood events, illustrating significant changes due to prolonged rainfall. While not all rivers in King County have assigned flood phases, they are monitored for flood risk. Once the floodwaters recede, the King County Flood Control District will issue a Flood Event Report detailing river levels, impacts, and response actions. In the meantime, the focus remains on public safety and effective communication.
