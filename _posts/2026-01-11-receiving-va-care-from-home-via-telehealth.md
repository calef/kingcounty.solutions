  <h2>How VA virtual care helps a Long Island Veteran stay active, healthy, and independent</h2>



  <p>A Long Island Veteran who served in the Army during the late 1960’s has spent the past several decades navigating serious health challenges. VA’s telehealth care options have transformed the way he manages his care.</p>



  <p>“I had two strokes when I was 47,” he said. “Ever since then, all my care has been done through VA.”</p>



  <p>Over the years, traveling to medical appointments became harder, but through VA’s virtual care resources, he now receives most of his care from home, where he’s most comfortable.</p>



  <p>“Going back and forth [to appointments] was quite an effort—driving, parking, getting into the hospital,” he said. “Learning there was a home care option changed everything.”</p>



  <h2>Getting comfortable with technology</h2>



  <p>Like many Veterans, he wasn’t sure about using new technology. He jokingly considers himself electronically challenged—he doesn’t own a computer or cell phone. But his VA care team provided him with a tablet through the <a>Digital Divide Consult</a> to connect him with VA virtual care resources.</p>



  <p>“They got me set up and showed me how to use it,” he said. “Now I use it every week. Nothing challenges me anymore.”</p>



  <p>Through <a>VA Video Connect</a>, VA’s secure videoconferencing app, and the <a>VA Health Chat</a> mobile app, he stays in touch with VA staff from home, avoiding unnecessary travel.</p>



  <p>“It’s made things easier not just for me, but for my wife, too,” he said.</p>



  <p>He also received adaptive equipment through VA’s <a>Home-Based Primary Care program</a>, including a chair lift for his stairs. “It’s the best thing in the world. I can get up and down without worrying,” he said.</p>



  <h2>Moving more with virtual chair yoga</h2>



  <p>The Long Island Veteran has also joined some virtual chair yoga classes offered at no cost through VA’s partnership with <a>Veteran’s Yoga Project</a>. He appreciates the easy pace at which they are taught, and he’s noticed real health benefits.</p>



  <p>“I was a mouth breather,” he explained. “Now they’ve taught me to breathe in through my nose and out through my mouth. It’s very helpful.”</p>



  <p>Looking back, he credits VA programs for helping him stay active and independent.</p>



  <p>“Without the support I get from VA, I don’t know where I’d be,” he said. “Everyone has been very good to me. I’m lucky.”</p>



  <p>To learn more about telehealth at VA, <a>visit the VA Telehealth Services website</a>.</p>

A Long Island Veteran, who served in the Army during the late 1960s, has successfully managed serious health challenges through VA telehealth services. After suffering two strokes at age 47, the Veteran found it increasingly difficult to attend in-person medical appointments. VA’s virtual care options have allowed him to receive most of his care from home, significantly easing the burden of travel. Initially hesitant about technology, he received a tablet from his VA care team, enabling him to connect with healthcare staff through VA Video Connect and the VA Health Chat app. This development has not only simplified his care but also benefited his wife. Additionally, he has participated in virtual chair yoga classes through a partnership with the Veteran’s Yoga Project, reporting improved breathing techniques and overall health. He expresses gratitude for the support and adaptive equipment provided by the VA, which has helped him maintain his independence and activity levels. He credits VA programs for his well-being, emphasizing the positive impact they have had on his life.
