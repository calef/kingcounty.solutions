  <h2>Acute low back pain didn’t stop this Veteran from restoring her independence</h2>



  <p>Army Veteran Vivian De Tiege first visited <a>VA Gulf Coast clinic</a> following an acute episode of low back pain with radicular symptoms that disrupted her ability to work around the house and engage in activities she loved. She lived alone and experienced discomfort while painting and doing yard work.</p>



  <p>“I want to know if this will get better,” De Tiege asked on her initial visit. </p>



  <p>The flare-up brought her to the emergency room, where she was diagnosed with “sciatica.” But the answers weren’t clear—and neither was the path forward.</p>



  <h2>A plan built on support</h2>



  <p>De Tiege’s physical therapy journey began with education and reassurance. Her care team explained the nature of acute low back pain and emphasized how most cases improve over time with the right approach.</p>



  <p>Her plan of care focused on core and hip strengthening, functional movement and pacing strategies. As her symptoms fluctuated, the team adapted her exercises—always balancing safety with her desire to return to the activities that mattered to her. Even after a fall that left her temporarily using a walker, De Tiege returned to therapy ready to work.</p>



  <h2>Progress that matters</h2>



  <p>With each session, De Tiege grew stronger and more confident. Her Functional Gait Assessment placed her above the fall risk threshold, and she steadily improved her ability to move independently.</p>



  <p>She completed exercises on the seated crosstrainer, practiced hip abduction and hamstring mobility, and regained trust in her body. Her Pain, Enjoyment of Life, General Activity (PEG) scores remained consistent, but her functional goals—like painting and gardening—were once again within reach.</p>



  <p>“I’m back to doing everything I want to do,” said De Tiege.</p>



  <h2>A confident return to life</h2>



  <p>De Tiege now reports minimal pain and has resumed all her desired daily activities. Her physical therapy journey wasn’t defined by perfect metrics, but by real-life milestones: the ability to walk unassisted, enjoy her home and live independently.</p>



  <p>“I’m good,” said De Tiege. “And if I need you, I know how to find you.”</p>



  <p>It’s never too late to apply for VA health care you’ve earned. If you are a Veteran or know a Veteran who has not yet applied for their VA health care benefits, learn how to <a>get started</a>.</p>

Army Veteran Vivian De Tiege overcame acute low back pain with the help of physical therapy at the VA Gulf Coast clinic. Initially, her pain disrupted her daily activities and left her questioning if she would improve. After being diagnosed with sciatica, she received education and reassurance from her care team, who emphasized that most cases of acute low back pain improve over time. Her treatment plan included core and hip strengthening, functional movement, and pacing strategies, which were adjusted as her symptoms changed. Despite a setback from a fall that required her to use a walker, De Tiege remained committed to her recovery. Over time, she became stronger and more confident, achieving significant progress in her mobility. Her Functional Gait Assessment indicated reduced fall risk, and she regained the ability to engage in activities like painting and gardening. De Tiege now reports minimal pain and has returned to her daily routines, stating, “I’m back to doing everything I want to do.” She highlights the importance of the support she received and recognizes that it’s never too late for Veterans to apply for the health care benefits they’ve earned.
