  <p><img src="https://s14621.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/poverty-action-main-image-801x566-1-361x255.jpg"></p><p>In this Washington, we take care of everyone.</p>
  <p>While the other Washington (DC) may have turned its back on those Americans who have the least to benefit the ultra-wealthy, here in Washington state, we understand that positive outcomes require positive investments.</p>
  <p><span><img src="https://s14621.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/groceries-no-background-1-400x.png">In our </span><span>Washington</span><span>, we know that by supporting people to meet</span><span>ing</span><span> their basic needs, we can build a stable foundation for everyone to thrive.</span><span> But with deep poverty on the rise, we must contend with federal funding cuts meant to gut lifeline programs and place blame on people living in poverty. </span><span> </span></p>
  <p><span>In our prosperous state, scarcity is a myth and poverty is a policy issue. Therefore, we’re committed to spending money on support instead of skepticism. In this Washington, we’ll fight to leverage progressive revenue and investments in robust social services to support our families and neighbors.</span><span> </span></p>
  <p><span>Solid Ground’s advocacy partner, <a>Statewide Poverty Action Network</a>, will work hard this legislative session to make sure state lawmakers maintain full support for lifeline programs:</span><span> </span></p>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <b><span>Pass progressive revenue</span></b><span> to preserve crucial programs with fewer federal dollars and </span><b><span>prevent cuts to lifeline services like SNAP and Medicaid</span></b><span>.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <b><span>Pass HB 2100: The Well Washington Fund</span></b><span> to meet the current shortfall created by HR1 (the federal administration’s so-called “One Big Beautiful Bill”).</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Increase staffing and technological capacity to </span><b><span>support growing human services demands</span></b><span> amplified by HR1.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Lower administrative costs and root programs in dignity and trust by</span><b><span> minimizing red tape and burdensome requirements</span></b><span>.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Bolster existing consumer protections and </span><b><span>protect low-income consumers from new predatory lending efforts</span></b><span>.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <p><span>Learn more about what we’re fighting for in Poverty Action’s </span><a><b><i><span>2026 Policy Agenda: It Takes a Village</span></i></b></a><span>, including our main policy priorities: <a>Meeting Basic Needs</a>, <a>Direct Cash</a>, and <a>Changing the Narrative on Poverty</a>. </span></p>

Washington state aims to support its residents in the face of rising poverty and federal funding cuts. Unlike the federal government, which has reduced support for those in need, state advocates emphasize the importance of investing in social services to help families thrive. The Statewide Poverty Action Network plans to push for legislation this session to maintain funding for essential programs like SNAP and Medicaid. Key initiatives include passing progressive revenue measures to offset federal cuts, implementing HB 2100 to address funding shortfalls, and enhancing service capacities to meet increasing demands. Advocates also seek to reduce administrative burdens on social services, strengthen consumer protections, and combat predatory lending practices. The Poverty Action team encourages residents to engage with lawmakers and participate in upcoming events, such as the MLK Lobby Day on January 19, 2026, to promote their agenda focused on meeting basic needs, direct cash assistance, and changing the narrative around poverty.
