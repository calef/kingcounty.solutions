The donkey population in Europe has decreased significantly due to mechanization, but recent interest in donkey milk has led to a slight resurgence in numbers. A review examined the husbandry, management, and welfare of donkeys (_Equus asinus_) in European Union member states, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom. Using the PRISMA technique, researchers screened 797 records, ultimately retaining 97 relevant studies. Most research focused on dairy donkeys in Italy, typically managed under extensive to semi-intensive systems. Donkeys involved in therapeutic interventions are usually managed semi-intensively. Generally, donkeys have access to shelter and outdoor spaces, though some graze freely year-round. However, health issues such as obesity, dental problems, and hoof disorders are often overlooked, impacting their welfare. Feeding practices are traditionally based on forages and concentrates, but many donkeys are overweight, except for lactating females, which tend to be underweight. This suggests a need for more balanced feeding strategies. Enhancing knowledge of donkey housing and feeding is crucial for developing evidence-based welfare guidelines that address the specific needs of the species.
