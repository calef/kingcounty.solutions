  Marc, our Community Engagement Manager, on a 1-minute tour of the shelter: </u></strong></span><a><span><strong><u>https://youtu.be/UIKjHLi1kjA</u></strong></span></a></p>\n<p>For
  the second consecutive winter, LCSNW is providing space and operational support
  for the Bellingham Severe Weather Shelter. The former Central Lutheran Church property,
  acquired by LCSNW two years ago, has become a potentially life-saving beacon of
  hope for people experiencing homelessness on nights when the temperature dips to
  32 degrees or colder.</p>\n<p>The Severe Weather Shelter had recorded several noteworthy
  numbers as of Feb. 12th, with several weeks of unpredictable northern Washington
  winter still to come:</p>\n<ul>\n<li>\n<p><strong>13 nights open</strong> through
  Feb. 12th</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>710 individual bed nights</strong> for guests</p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>64
  guests receiving wound or medical care</strong></p>\n</li>\n<li>\n<p><strong>Over
  1,400 meals</strong> served</p>\n</li>\n</ul>\n<p>The weather has generally been
  warmer in early February this year, though the shelter was open the nights of Feb.
  10th and 11th and is now set to be open the nights of Feb. 14-18. There’s also a
  Bellingham Day Shelter (on a different site, not affiliated with LCSNW) that is
  open on days when temperatures are projected to reach freezing. Whatcom County officials
  announce shelter openings several days in advance, based on weather forecasts, on
  their Facebook page: <a>https://www.facebook.com/WhatcomCountyHealth/</a></p>\n<p>Marc
  Hander is LCSNW’s Community Engagement Manager at the Bellingham site. Marc’s duties
  include attending the Whatcom County shelter leadership and staff meetings, supporting
  staff, engaging in community meetings, and monitoring neighbor relations. He also
  worked two shifts at the shelter during a stretch of sub-freezing nights in January.</p>\n<p>“My
  experience and learning last year give me a ‘seasoned’ status, and I’m able to support
  staff with confidence,” Marc said.</p>\n<p>The LCSNW space can hold approximately
  60 guests each night, all adults; the county offers accommodations for youth and
  families at a different location. Guests are allowed to bring their service, emotional
  support or therapy animals.</p>\n<p>In addition to providing emergency shelter space
  under a contract with Whatcom County, the Bellingham property serves as <a>the northernmost
  outpost of LCSNW’s Santa for Seniors program</a>. In the next few years, Compass
  Housing Alliance plans to begin developing 200 affordable housing units there.</p>\n<p>For
  now, LCSNW is pleased to offer health, justice, and hope in a unique way: by helping
  to shelter some of the region’s most vulnerable people during the harshest season
  of the year.</p>\n</div>\n\t\t\t</div>\n\t\t\t</div>\n<div>\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t<div>\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t<span><img

LCSNW is once again operating the Bellingham Severe Weather Shelter this winter, providing crucial support for individuals experiencing homelessness during cold nights. Located at the former Central Lutheran Church property, the shelter is open when temperatures drop to 32 degrees or lower. As of February 12, the shelter has recorded 13 nights of operation, accommodating 710 individual bed nights, offering medical care to 64 guests, and serving over 1,400 meals. Although warmer weather has been noted in early February, the shelter was open on February 10 and 11 and will operate from February 14 to 18. The Bellingham Day Shelter also provides daytime services during freezing temperatures. Whatcom County officials announce shelter openings in advance on their Facebook page. The LCSNW shelter can accommodate around 60 adults each night, allowing guests to bring their service animals. In addition to emergency shelter, the property also supports LCSNW’s Santa for Seniors program. Future plans include developing 200 affordable housing units at the site by Compass Housing Alliance, while LCSNW continues to provide essential services to vulnerable populations during the winter months.
