  <p> It’s always fascinating to compile The Seattle Public Library’s top checkouts for the year to get a sense of what’s been on the minds of Seattle’s readers. In 2025, they turned closer to home and explored local voices — perhaps not surprising given the state of national dialogue.</p>



  <p>Several of our most checked-out print books were written by local authors, including Seattle Times journalist Moira Macdonald’s debut novel <a>“Storybook Ending”</a>; travel writer Rick Steves’ memoir <a>“On the Hippie Trail”</a>; and David B. Williams’ “Wild in Seattle.”</p>



  <p>A little further afield, Spokane author Jess Walter’s new novel <a>“So Far Gone”</a> also ranked as a top print checkout, and Oregon writer Omar El Akkad’s “One Day, Everyone Will Have Always Been Against This” was in the top 10 for nonfiction print books. Bainbridge Island author Kristin Hannah’s <a>“The Women”</a> ranked high for e-book and audiobook checkouts.</p>



  <p>Of course, Seattle library patrons also followed national reading trends, checking out bestsellers such as Anne Tyler’s “Three Days in June,” Percival Everett’s Pulitzer Prize-winning “James” and Rebecca Yarros’ wildly popular romantasy “Onyx Storm” thousands of times.</p>



  <p>Another takeaway: Seattle readers want compelling stories, which can mean looking to older titles with a lasting impact. The audiobook version of “Braiding Sweetgrass” by Robin Wall Kimmerer, originally published in 2013, has been the top checked-out book of any format for the past three years.</p>



  <p>Below are the top 10 checkouts from The Seattle Public Library in five categories for 2025, from Jan. 1 through Nov. 30 (data does not include renewals). As always, SPL values and protects patron privacy, and all checkout data is anonymous.</p>

The Seattle Public Library's most popular checkouts for 2025 reflect a strong interest in local voices and compelling stories. Notably, several top print books were authored by local writers, including Moira Macdonald's debut novel "Storybook Ending," Rick Steves' memoir "On the Hippie Trail," and David B. Williams' "Wild in Seattle." Spokane's Jess Walter and Oregon's Omar El Akkad also made the list with their respective works. Kristin Hannah's "The Women" was among the top e-book and audiobook checkouts. Seattle readers gravitated toward national bestsellers like Anne Tyler's "Three Days in June" and Percival Everett's Pulitzer Prize-winning "James." The audiobook "Braiding Sweetgrass" by Robin Wall Kimmerer, published in 2013, was the most checked-out book across all formats for three consecutive years.

The top checkouts from various categories include:

- **Adult Fiction (Physical)**: "Three Days in June" by Anne Tyler (3,164 checkouts)
- **Adult Fiction (E-books)**: "Onyx Storm" by Rebecca Yarros (4,883)
- **Adult Nonfiction (Physical)**: "Abundance" by Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson (2,784)
- **Adult Nonfiction (E-books)**: "Maybe You Should Talk to Someone" by Lori Gottlieb (2,979)
- **Audiobooks**: "Braiding Sweetgrass" by Robin Wall Kimmerer (5,464)
