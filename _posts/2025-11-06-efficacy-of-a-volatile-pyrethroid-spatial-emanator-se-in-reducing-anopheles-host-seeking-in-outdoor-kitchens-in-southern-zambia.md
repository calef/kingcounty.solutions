title: Efficacy of a volatile pyrethroid spatial emanator (SE) in reducing Anopheles

In southern Zambia, malaria transmission is primarily driven by outdoor-biting Anopheles mosquitoes, which pose a risk to people cooking in open-walled kitchen shelters. This study evaluated the effectiveness of volatile pyrethroid spatial emanators (SE) that emit the insecticide transfluthrin to protect these kitchens. Forty-one households participated, with local mosquito collectors trained to conduct human landing collections (HLC) from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. over 15 weeks. A total of 3,021 mosquitoes were collected, with Anopheles species comprising about half. The study found that mosquito activity was lower in kitchens protected by SE devices, which reduced host-seeking behavior by approximately 65-70% initially, but this effectiveness diminished to about 20-25% after four weeks. The highest mosquito activity occurred in the middle of the night, while SE devices did not significantly impact activity between 6 p.m. and 9 p.m. The results indicate that SE devices can provide effective nighttime protection against mosquitoes, although their efficacy decreases over time, suggesting the need for further investigation into their use.
