The article discusses the side effects and expectations associated with the abortion pill, which consists of two medications: mifepristone and misoprostol. Mifepristone, taken first, blocks progesterone, leading to common side effects like abdominal pain, cramping, nausea, and headache within 2 to 24 hours. Misoprostol, taken 24 to 48 hours later, induces uterine contractions, resulting in intense cramping and heavy bleeding, often accompanied by passing large blood clots. While some women may experience heavy bleeding for weeks, the most severe symptoms typically occur within 24 hours after taking misoprostol.

The article highlights that the complication rate for the abortion pill is four times higher than for surgical abortions, with risks increasing with gestational age. Serious complications may include incomplete abortion and severe infections. Additionally, emotional and mental health effects can arise post-abortion, with symptoms ranging from relief to sadness, anxiety, and PTSD. Women with prior mental health issues may be more susceptible to these feelings.

For any concerning side effects, such as excessive bleeding or fever, seeking emergency medical attention is advised. Support resources are available for those needing emotional assistance following an abortion.
