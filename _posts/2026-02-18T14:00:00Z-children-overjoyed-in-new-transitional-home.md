Marine Corps veteran Ebony Waters faced significant challenges after her military discharge, including physical limitations, an abusive relationship, and financial struggles that forced her and her three young children into an unsafe apartment. After a year of searching, she found a home through Operation Homefront's Transitional Homes for Veterans (THV) program, which helps veterans live rent-free for two to three years to stabilize their finances and careers. Since its inception in 2018, the program has assisted 49 families and saved them over $2.2 million in mortgage costs.

Waters moved into a three-bedroom home in Fuquay-Varina, North Carolina, in October, excited to provide a safer environment for her children. Previously, they shared one bed in a moldy apartment. With support from donors, Waters has been able to create a stable home, expressing her deep gratitude for the opportunity. She hopes to establish roots in the community, where her children can thrive. Having originally joined the Marines to achieve stability and set an example, Waters remains committed to improving her family's situation and is optimistic about their future.
