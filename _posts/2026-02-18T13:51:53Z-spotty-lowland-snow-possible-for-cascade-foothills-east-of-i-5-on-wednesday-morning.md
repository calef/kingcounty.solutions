Scattered lowland snow is possible in western Washington on Wednesday morning, particularly in Snohomish, King, and Pierce counties, due to snow levels around 500 feet and overnight temperatures in the upper 20s to low 30s. Brief bursts of snow may occur, especially in foothill communities and higher terrains east of Puget Sound. Early reports indicated light snow in areas like Marysville and Lake Stevens, although coverage was inconsistent. The Granite Falls Schools District has canceled classes due to the weather conditions. 

As of Tuesday night, KING 5 activated its First Alert for this weather event, which could impact travel and safety in the region. Earlier on Tuesday, light wet snow was reported on the Olympic Peninsula and in isolated locations near Kent, with trace amounts at Sea-Tac. Most of this snow melted quickly as temperatures remained above freezing. Accumulation is expected to be minor, primarily affecting side roads and elevated surfaces during the morning commute. By midmorning, temperatures are expected to rise above freezing, turning any remaining precipitation back to rain. A warming trend is anticipated later in the week, ending the chances for lowland snow.
