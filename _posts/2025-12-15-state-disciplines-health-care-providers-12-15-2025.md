  department’s Health Systems Quality Assurance Division works with boards, commissions,
  and advisory committees to set licensing standards for more than 80 health care
  professions (e.g., dentists, nurses, counselors). Information about disciplinary
  action taken against medical doctors and physician assistants can be found on the
  <a>Washington Medical Commission (WMC) website</a>. Questions about WMC disciplinary
  actions can be sent to <a>media@wmc.wa.gov</a>.</p>\n\n<p>Information about health

  and renewal date of their credential, disciplinary actions and copies of legal documents

  to call and report their complaint.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Chelan County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  October 2025 the Department of Health denied <strong>Candice A. Haase’s</strong>

  allegedly accepted money from a client.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Clark County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  November 2025 the Department of Health granted <strong>Erin Maureen Vallejo Lopez’s</strong>
  (DT61006123) application to extend the term of her apprentice dispensing optician
  credential by two years. Vallejo Lopez was issued her registration in 2019, which
  has since expired and cannot be renewed. Her request for an extension showed she
  was unable to obtain licensure within the required six years for reasons beyond
  her control.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Douglas County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In November 2025

  NC61493818, NA61388002) with unprofessional conduct. In April 2024, Sprauer allegedly
  failed to respond to a patient’s medication request, showed erratic behavior at
  work and was found responsible for narcotic and log discrepancies. He was subsequently
  terminated by his employer. In May 2022 and January 2024, Sprauer also was convicted
  of driving under the influence and violating an ignition interlock requirement,
  respectively, both gross misdemeanors. He did not respond to the department’s request
  for information.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Ferry County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In November

  (HM61255007, NC61542908) from 2024 and 2025 agreed orders and terminated compliance
  monitoring for both.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Grant County</strong></h2>\n\n<p><a>In November

The Washington State Department of Health has taken disciplinary actions against various health care providers as of December 15, 2025. Among those disciplined, Candice A. Haase had her home care aide application denied after allegedly accepting money from a client. Ryan Robert Sprauer faced charges for unprofessional conduct due to erratic behavior and drug-related issues, while James Kamau Kirumba was charged for physically assaulting a patient. Other actions included the termination of probation for Cassandra Nichole Lembcke and Cristian Alexis Guzman, as well as an agreed order reinstating Amanda Jean Pelot's credentials.

The department also issued cease-and-desist orders to individuals practicing massage therapy without credentials. Notable cases involved Colleta Nambuzi, who allegedly submitted false training certifications, and Tierra Lynn Talmadge, charged with diverting controlled substances. Additionally, the Chiropractic Quality Assurance Commission withdrew charges against Jorge Antonio Barrientos-Lopez, while dental assistant Julie Evette Fernandez faced charges for theft. Health care providers charged with unprofessional conduct have 20 days to respond, after which the case enters the settlement process or proceeds to a hearing.
