The Bellevue City Council recently honored Conrad Lee, its longest-serving councilmember, who is stepping down after more than three decades of service. First elected in 1993, Lee has held various leadership roles, including mayor and deputy mayor. Councilmembers praised his dedication to public service and transparent decision-making.

In other business, the council adopted the Diversity Advantage Plan 2035, which outlines a strategic approach to diversity, equity, and inclusion for the next decade. The unanimously approved plan reflects community and staff priorities aligned with existing city goals.

Additionally, the council updated its critical areas development regulations to meet state requirements by the Dec. 31 deadline. The updates enhance protections for wetlands, streams, and wildlife habitats while supporting Bellevue’s growth objectives.

The council also held a special meeting with state legislators to discuss Bellevue’s 2026 legislative agenda and share priorities for the upcoming session, which begins Jan. 12. The meeting allowed local officials to advocate for projects important to the city.

The next city council meeting is scheduled for Jan. 6.
