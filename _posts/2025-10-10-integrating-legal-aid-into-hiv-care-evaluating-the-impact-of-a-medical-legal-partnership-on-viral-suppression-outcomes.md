A study evaluated the impact of Medical-Legal Partnerships (MLPs) on HIV care adherence and viral suppression among people with HIV (PWH) in Philadelphia. MLPs aim to address social and structural determinants of health, such as legal issues related to housing and employment, that can hinder access to care. In the trial, two matched service organizations were assigned to either MLP or treatment-as-usual (TAU). The MLP intervention included training, legal screenings by peer navigators, and integration of legal services. A total of 202 participants were enrolled through various outreach methods.

Results showed that participants in the MLP group achieved undetectable viral loads faster than those receiving TAU. At the 3-month follow-up, MLP participants had a significantly higher likelihood of viral suppression compared to the TAU group (odds ratio = 2.42). While the 6-month follow-up showed a reduced odds ratio of 2.28, it did not reach statistical significance. These findings suggest that integrating legal services into HIV care can enhance health outcomes and reduce disparities among PWH by addressing legal barriers to care.
