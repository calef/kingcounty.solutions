The deadliest avalanche in U.S. history occurred on June 20, 1981, on Mount Rainier in Washington state, resulting in the deaths of 11 climbers. A large section of the Ingraham Glacier fractured, sending ice and snow down onto a climbing party consisting of 10 amateur climbers and their guide. Larry St. Peter, a survivor, recalled hearing a crack followed by a collective gasp from the group as the avalanche struck. Rescue efforts were hampered by severe weather, delaying search operations until June 22, when crews were unable to locate any victims, leading to the search being called off on June 23. This tragedy remains the most lethal avalanche incident in recent U.S. history. Comparatively, the most recent deadly avalanche occurred near Lake Tahoe, where eight bodies were recovered after backcountry skiers were caught in a similar disaster, marking the deadliest avalanche in the U.S. since 1981. Washington state has seen many fatal avalanches, with an average of 25 to 30 avalanche-related deaths occurring annually across the country, according to the National Avalanche Center.
