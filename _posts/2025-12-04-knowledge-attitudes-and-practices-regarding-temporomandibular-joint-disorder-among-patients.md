A study conducted at Shenzhen University General Hospital examined the knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) of patients with temporomandibular joint disorder (TMD). The research, carried out from October to November 2024, involved analyzing 229 valid questionnaire responses. The majority of participants, 72.9%, were female. The results showed that 47.2% of respondents reported no depression, while 87.8% experienced no or mild anxiety. Mean scores indicated a lack of adequate knowledge, with knowledge scored at 8.59, attitude at 38.46, and practice at 40.37. Positive correlations were found between knowledge and attitude, knowledge and practice, and attitude and practice. Structural equation modeling revealed significant direct effects of knowledge and attitude on practice. The study highlights that patients with TMD exhibit insufficient knowledge and negative attitudes, indicating a need for improved education and attitude enhancement. Recommendations include integrating tailored patient education programs and behavioral counseling into routine TMD management to boost treatment adherence, symptom control, and overall quality of life.
