The conversation addressed why milestones are important and emphasized the idea that children grow and develop at their own rate.</p>
  <p>Below, Emily provides a list of common milestones sorted by general age ranges and encourages caregivers to give their child until the end of each range to meet the milestone. If your child is not meeting milestones by this time, consider talking to your healthcare provider or reaching out to the Encompass Early Support for Infants and Toddlers (ESIT) team for a free evaluation.</p>
  <p><strong>Motor Development</strong></p>
  <ul>
  <li>Roll tummy to back and back to tummy (4-6 months)</li>
  <li>Sit without support (6-9 months)</li>
  <li>Crawl (9-12 months)</li>
  <li>Pull to stand and cruise along furniture (9-12 months)</li>
  <li>Takes several independent steps (12-15 months)</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Communication Development</strong></p>
  <ul>
  <li>Coos and smiles (0-2 months)</li>
  <li>Makes different vowel sounds (2-4 months)</li>
  <li>Turns head towards a sound or voice (2-4 months)</li>
  <li>Begins to giggle or laugh (4-6 months)</li>
  <li>Combines consonants and vowels in different ways (6-9 months)</li>
  <li>Uses simple gestures (6-9 months)</li>
  <li>Imitates speech sounds (9-12 months)</li>
  <li>Meaningfully uses “mama” and “dada” (9-12 months)</li>
  <li>Pays attention to where you are looking and pointing (9-12 months)</li>
  <li>Points to objects they want (12-15 months)</li>
  <li>Uses 1 or 2 words other than mama and dada (12-15 months)</li>
  <li>Imitates simple words and actions (15-18 months)</li>
  <li>Follows simple directions (15-18 months)</li>
  <li>Says 3 or more words (15-18 months)</li>
  <li>Names objects and pictures (18-24 months)</li>
  <li>Begins to use two-word phrases (18-24 months)</li>
  <li>Uses at least 50 words (24-30 months)</li>
  <li>Asks “what” and “where” questions (30-36 months)</li>
  </ul>
  <p><strong>Feeding Development</strong></p>
  <ul>
  <li>Begins to eat baby-safe foods such as purees (4-6 months)</li>
  <li>Able to eat soft finger foods/table foods (6-9 months)</li>
  <li>Attempts to finger feed self (6-9 months)</li>
  <li>Uses a spoon (18-24 months)</li>
  <li>Uses a fork (30-36 months)</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Gauging milestones can be tricky. During our podcast, Emily mentioned that sometimes, caregivers just have a gut feeling that something isn&#8217;t tracking. If you have that feeling, or your child is not meeting a milestone by the end of the listed range, our ESIT team encourages you to seek a screening. All families with children aged birth to 3 are eligible for a full developmental evaluation at no cost to the family.  Reach out to our team at 425.888.3347 or email <a href="mailto:esit@encompassnw.org">esit@encompassnw.org</a>.</p>

The article discusses common developmental milestones for children and the importance of tracking these milestones as indicators of healthy growth. Encompass Developmental Specialist Emily Arkus emphasizes that children develop at their own pace and encourages caregivers to allow until the end of each milestone age range before seeking help. Key milestones are categorized into motor, communication, and feeding development, with specific ages outlined for various skills. For example, by 4-6 months, infants should be able to roll over and coo, while by 12-15 months, they should be taking independent steps and using simple words. If caregivers have concerns about their child's development, especially if milestones are not met by the end of the suggested range, they are advised to consult a healthcare provider or contact the Encompass Early Support for Infants and Toddlers (ESIT) team for a free evaluation. The article provides a reassurance that families with children aged birth to 3 can access developmental evaluations at no cost. For more resources and support, caregivers are encouraged to visit relevant websites or reach out directly via provided contact information.
