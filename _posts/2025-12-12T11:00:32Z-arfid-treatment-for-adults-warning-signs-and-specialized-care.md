Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder (ARFID) is a serious eating disorder that affects many adults, often misclassified as simple "picky eating." It is characterized by significantly restricted food intake due to sensory sensitivities, fear of negative consequences like choking or nausea, and lack of interest in eating. Common signs include anxiety around food, reliance on a limited list of "safe" foods, and extensive meal planning, which can severely impact overall health and daily life.

Despite its serious nature, ARFID often goes unrecognized in adults, partly due to its late recognition as an official eating disorder in 2013. Signs can be dismissed as simple preferences or labeled as picky eating, complicating diagnosis and treatment. Specialized care is crucial, as traditional eating disorder programs may not adequately address the unique aspects of ARFID.

The Emily Program has established the ARFID Center of Excellence, set to open in January 2026 in Raleigh-Durham, North Carolina, as the first residential treatment facility solely for adults with ARFID. This center will provide tailored support, focusing on restoring nutritional health, reducing anxiety, and expanding food variety through structured, individualized treatment methods.
