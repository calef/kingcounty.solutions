  <p>Like all of the Emerald City, The Seattle Public Library has been reveling in the Seahawks’ Super Bowl win — but in a very library-like way.</p>



  <p><span>Late last week, we placed a </span><a><span>bookish bet </span></a><span>with Boston Public Library: The library with the winning team would get to create a booklist for the other to host. After Sunday’s victory, our librarians created a fun (and very pointed) list: Called </span><a><b><span>“Seattle Picks for Patriots Fans,”</span></b></a><span> the 35 titles celebrate Seattle’s cultural and natural riches, sports history and, of course, the joy of winning.  </span></p>



  <p> </p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Bookish-bet.jpg?resize=311%2C359&amp;ssl=1"></a></figure>



  <p><span>Books include “</span><a><span>Inflation</span></a><span>,” by political economists Mark Blyth and Nicolö Fraccaroli; </span><a><span>“Broken Horses,”</span></a><span> by Seattle music superstar Brandi Carlile; “</span><a><span>Things That Make White People Uncomfortable</span></a><i><span>,” </span></i><span>by former Seattle Seahawks defensive end Michael Bennett; </span><a><span>“Comeuppance Served Cold,”</span></a><span> a Seattle noir fantasy by Marion Deeds; and </span><a><span>“Loser,”</span></a><span> by Clark Humphrey.</span></p>



  <p>Of course, our Seattle patrons are welcome to read any of these books as well to keep the celebration going.</p>



  <p><span>Boston Public Library was a good sport, </span><a><span>congratulating</span></a><span> Seattle on its win, sharing the booklist link in its Instagram bio, and noting that “though we are divided by football, we are always on the same team in supporting the freedom to read!”  </span></p>



  <p><span>The Seattle Public Library and Boston Public Library are both part of </span><a><span>Books Unbanned</span></a><span>, providing free e-cards to young people across the country so they can read what they like from the libraries’ respective digital collections. In October, the Books Unbanned coalition </span><a><span>announced</span> </a><span>it had collectively provided over 51,000 digital library cards and that more than 1 million e-books have been checked out through the programs. </span></p>



  <h3>A library seat at the Super Bowl parade</h3>



  <p><span>Speaking of keeping the celebration going, yesterday was an especially fun day to work at the Central Library (once you made it there — shout-out to King County Metro and Sound Transit). </span></p>



  <p><span>Starting in the early hours of the morning, the excited crowd waiting along Fourth Avenue in front the of the Library grew and grew and grew.</span></p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Warren-Chin-Hawks-parade.png?resize=1024%2C931&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>The Central Library goes very well with blue and green. Photo by Warren Chin. </figcaption></figure>



  <p><span>Once the Library opened its doors at 10 a.m., some parade goers began to explore some of the choicest observation spots from the highest levels of the building. “It’s my go-to spot for parades,” one patron shared. (Sshhhh ….)</span></p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Central-Seahawks-parade-watching.jpg?resize=768%2C1024&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>With a zoom camera, an upper level of the Central Library was a prime parade watching spot for this Seattle Times photographer.</figcaption></figure>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/9th-floor-watching-Elisa-M.jpeg?resize=1024%2C768&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Some people settled in on Level 9 for work and parade-waiting.</figcaption></figure>



  <p>Our staff — who had their own choice spots — kindly shared some of their snaps from the day, in between showing people to restrooms, the cafe stand on Level 3, and other amenities.</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Seahawks-float.jpeg?resize=1024%2C683&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Up close to the parade. </figcaption></figure>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Watching-with-feet.jpeg?resize=1024%2C768&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Watching the parade from Spring Street, above and below. </figcaption></figure>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/IMG_6724.jpeg?resize=768%2C1024&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>A bus stop can serve other purposes. </figcaption></figure>



  <p>And well after the last float trundled down Fourth Avenue, the chanting floated up through all 11 levels of the library. “Sea … hawks … Sea … hawks … Sea … HAWKS!”</p>



  <figure><a><img src="https://i0.wp.com/blog.spl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Seahawks-Float-Charles-W.jpg?resize=1000%2C666&amp;ssl=1"></a><figcaption>Chanting floated up all 11 levels of the Central Library until the last float went by. Photo by Charles Wesley.</figcaption></figure>



  <p>Congratulations, Seahawks and Seattle! We needed this. </p>



  <p>Now, you’ve got <a>some reading</a> to do!</p>



  <p><em>– Elisa M., Communications</em></p>

The Seattle Public Library celebrated the Seahawks' Super Bowl victory by winning a friendly bet with the Boston Public Library. The wager involved creating a booklist for the opposing library. After the win, Seattle's librarians crafted a list titled “Seattle Picks for Patriots Fans,” featuring 35 titles that highlight Seattle's culture and sports history. Notable books include “Inflation” by Mark Blyth and Nicolö Fraccaroli, “Broken Horses” by Brandi Carlile, and “Things That Make White People Uncomfortable” by Michael Bennett. Boston Public Library graciously congratulated Seattle and shared the list on Instagram, emphasizing their shared commitment to supporting the freedom to read.

Following the victory, the Central Library in Seattle served as a prime viewing spot for the Super Bowl parade, attracting a large crowd. The library opened at 10 a.m., allowing parade-goers to take advantage of its high vantage points. Staff members shared photos of the celebration, which included enthusiastic chanting from library patrons. The event reinforced community spirit and the joy of reading, reminding everyone to enjoy the recommended titles as part of the celebration.
