  <h2>Bringing care home</h2>



  <p>Rural Veterans who need a new brace or prosthesis are aware of the barriers to care. It can mean planning for a long drive to your local VA, hoping traffic isn’t too bad, and bracing yourself for hours in waiting rooms. Sometimes, you must ask a friend or family member to take the day off just to get you there. For a lot of Veterans, the hassle meant putting off care or skipping it altogether.</p>



  <p>That’s exactly why the <a>Mobile Prosthetic and Orthotic Care (MoPOC)</a> truck exists. It’s not just a vehicle. It’s a promise that you matter, no matter your ZIP code.</p>



  <h2>A clinic that comes to you</h2>


  <div>
  <figure><a><img src="https://news.va.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/01/MoPOC-Px-BK_r1-scaled.jpg?w=730"></a><figcaption>Photo of the Orthotics and Prosthetics workshop that is inside of the MoPOC truck along with Army Veteran James Turners prosthetic leg that is getting a tune up. </figcaption></figure>
  </div>


  <p>With support from <a>VA’s Office of Rural Health</a>, the MoPOC program takes the full range of prosthetic and orthotic care on the road. Three times a week, the <a>MoPOC </a>team rolls into Tyler, Greenville and Granbury, Texas. It brings fittings, repairs and adjustments right to local clinics, so Veterans can get the help they need without leaving their communities.</p>



  <p>The MoPOC truck changed the life of Army Veteran James Turner. “Before this, I had to get my neighbor to drive me to Dallas every time I needed a fitting, and sometimes I’d have to wait a month just to be seen,” he shared. “Now, VA comes to me. I can walk better, I’m in less pain and I feel like I matter.”</p>



  <h2>Care that’s local, fast and personal</h2>



  <p>MoPOC means Veterans get appointments faster, closer to home and with people who understand what service really means. The numbers are impressive. More satisfied Veterans, better access and real savings for VA. But the real story is the impact on everyday life.</p>



  <p>Ligia Miguel-Ghaziosharif, the certified prosthetist and orthotist who travels with the truck, knows the difference this makes. An Air Force Veteran herself, she brings that same sense of mission to every appointment.</p>



  <p>“Every Veteran who steps onto the truck is more than just a patient, they’re family,” Miguel-Ghaziosharif said. “I know what it means to serve, and I know how much independence matters. Our goal is to give Veterans the freedom to live life on their own terms, wherever they call home.”</p>



  <h2>Building trust, one visit at a time</h2>


  <div>
  <figure><a><img src="https://news.va.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/01/MOPOC00071_r2.jpg?w=730"></a><figcaption>Certified Prosthetist-Orthotist (CPO) Ligia Miguel-Ghaziosharif, leader of the Mobile Prosthetic and Orthotic Care (MoPOC) truck at VA North Texas while she is working on adjusting and fixing a Veterans prosthetic leg in the trucks workshop.</figcaption></figure>
  </div>


  <p>Dr. Corinthus Pittman, who helped get MoPOC off the ground, believes meeting Veterans where they are is the real heart of the program. “This isn’t just about convenience,” Pittman explained. “It’s about honoring our commitment to Veterans by removing barriers to care. The MoPOC truck lets us meet Veterans where they are, and that means better outcomes, higher satisfaction and healthier lives.”</p>



  <p>Veterans themselves have said it best. In a recent <a>Journal of Rural Health</a> study, they reported not just high satisfaction with MoPOC, but real improvements in quality of life. For many, it’s the difference between feeling left behind and feeling seen.</p>



  <p>As the MoPOC truck travels the backroads of North Texas, it isn’t just delivering care. It’s delivering respect, dignity and the simple truth that every Veteran deserves to be remembered and cared for right where they are.</p>

Rural Veterans often face significant barriers when seeking prosthetic and orthotic care, including long drives to VA facilities and lengthy wait times. The Mobile Prosthetic and Orthotic Care (MoPOC) truck addresses these challenges by bringing services directly to Veterans in local communities. Operating three times a week in Tyler, Greenville, and Granbury, Texas, the MoPOC program provides fittings, repairs, and adjustments at local clinics, allowing Veterans to receive care without extensive travel.

Army Veteran James Turner shared how the service improved his life, reducing pain and providing him with a sense of importance. Certified prosthetist Ligia Miguel-Ghaziosharif, who travels with the truck, emphasizes the program's mission to enhance Veterans' independence and quality of life. Dr. Corinthus Pittman highlights that MoPOC is about more than convenience; it is a commitment to removing barriers to care, which leads to better health outcomes and increased satisfaction among Veterans. A recent study indicated high satisfaction levels and improved quality of life for users of the MoPOC service. The program aims to ensure that every Veteran feels respected and cared for, no matter where they live.
