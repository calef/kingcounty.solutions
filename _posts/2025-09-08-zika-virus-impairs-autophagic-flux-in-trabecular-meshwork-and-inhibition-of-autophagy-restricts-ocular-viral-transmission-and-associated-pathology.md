  <p><b>ABSTRACT</b></p>
  <p>Zika virus (ZIKV) is the lone member of Flavivirus family known to cause congenital glaucoma following <i>in utero</i> exposure. The molecular mechanisms of ZIKV-induced glaucoma remain elusive, with no known therapeutic modalities. Autophagy plays a dual role in viral infections and glaucoma pathogenesis. However, how ZIKV interferes with autophagic pathways in the trabecular meshwork (TM) and whether autophagy contributes to ZIKV-induced ocular complications remains elusive. Here, we investigated the role of autophagy in ZIKV ocular pathogenesis utilizing a primary human trabecular meshwork cells (HTMC) and IFNAR1<sup>-/-</sup> mouse model of ocular infection. Our results show that ZIKV permissively infects HTMC, elicits a dysregulated cytokine response, and triggers autophagic activity in TM and the anterior segment of the IFNAR1<sup>-/-</sup> mouse eyes. Although ZIKV initially activates autophagy in TM, it impairs the overall autophagic flux during infection by modulating the VPS39, a HOPS complex, and STX17, a SNARE complex protein, to inhibit autophagosomal maturation. ZIKV utilizes late endosomes/lysosomes for its replication and egress in TM. We further demonstrated that pharmacologic inhibition of autophagy at the autophagosomal-lysosomal fusion stage by hydroxychloroquine (HCQ), or bafilomycin A1 (Baf-A1), restricts viral replication in TM and ZIKV-induced ocular pathology in mice. Using RNA-seq analysis, we uncovered that autophagy modulation modulates ZIKV-altered host transcriptomes governing critical cellular pathways, including host immunity, antigen processing and presentation, and cell death. Our study is the first to demonstrate the mechanistic insight by which ZIKV manipulates autophagic activity in the ocular milieu, and precise modulation of autophagy could be a potential therapeutic avenue to treat or prevent ZIKV-induced ocular complications.</p>

Zika virus (ZIKV), a member of the Flavivirus family, is linked to congenital glaucoma following in utero exposure, but the mechanisms behind this connection are not well understood. Research shows that ZIKV infects human trabecular meshwork cells and alters cytokine responses while triggering autophagic activity in the trabecular meshwork and mouse eyes. Initially, ZIKV activates autophagy, but it ultimately disrupts the overall autophagic process by interfering with proteins involved in autophagosomal maturation. ZIKV relies on late endosomes and lysosomes for replication and release. The study found that inhibiting autophagy at the fusion stage between autophagosomes and lysosomes can reduce viral replication and mitigate ocular damage in mice. Additionally, RNA sequencing revealed that modulating autophagy affects ZIKV-altered host gene expression related to immunity, antigen presentation, and cell death. This research offers new insights into how ZIKV manipulates autophagic pathways in the eye and suggests that targeting autophagy may provide therapeutic strategies to treat or prevent ZIKV-related ocular issues.
