  class=\"field field--name-body field--type-text-with-summary field--label-hidden
  field--item\"><h2>Final planning study report published, work begins on preliminary
  design and environmental review&nbsp;</h2><p>CARBONADO – The Washington State Department
  of Transportation has published the final <a href=\"/construction-planning/search-studies/sr-165-carbon-river-fairfax-bridge-planning-study\">planning
  study</a> report that addresses access across the Carbon River Canyon. WSDOT accelerated
  the $1.5 million planning study following the closure of the bridge in April.&nbsp;</p><p>The
  agency is now turning its focus to the preliminary design and environmental review
  of two alternatives:</p><ul><li>Replace the State Route 165 Carbon River/Fairfax
  Bridge just north of the existing alignment.</li><li>Keep SR 165 closed and remove
  the Carbon River/Fairfax Bridge.</li></ul><p>WSDOT allocated approximately $7 million
  of preservation funding out of the 2025-27 biennium Transportation Budget for this
  next phase of work.</p><h3>Next steps</h3><p>Soil information is needed to determine
  where bridge foundations can be built. Survey crews are also active in the area
  gathering topographic information. This plus the soil data will be used to identify
  the best location and alignment for a bridge across the canyon.</p><p>“WSDOT is
  actively seeking right-of-way permits, which will allow crews to drill and collect
  information on soil and rock conditions in the project area,” said Olympic Region
  Administrator Steve Roark.</p><p>“This information and analysis help determine the
  type, size and location for a new bridge,” Roark added.</p><p>WSDOT estimates a
  six- to twelve-month timeline to collect the information. The timeline is dependent
  on securing environmental permits to remove some trees in the scenic Carbon River
  Canyon for drilling</p><h3>Environmental review</h3><p>The National Environmental
  Policy Act or NEPA, is a federal process which evaluates and addresses the project’s
  effect on land, water, air, wildlife and endangered species, nearby habitat, human
  health and historic sites.&nbsp;</p><p>“WSDOT is responsible for collecting and
  analyzing the project alternatives’ environmental impacts and then hands off the
  findings to other agencies for further review and comment,” said Roark.</p><p>Other
  agencies involved in the NEPA review include the Federal Highway Administration,
  US Fish and Wildlife, National Marine Fisheries Service, Department of Archaeology
  and Historic Preservation, Tribes and others. Each reviewer is allotted time to
  review and comment on WSDOT’s work, ranging from 30 to 135 days or more. Altogether,
  WSDOT is planning for a 24-month NEPA evaluation and review timeline.&nbsp;</p><p>The
  public will have opportunities to provide input during environmental review. WSDOT
  will announce those dates once the information is available.</p><p>Final steps to
  construction</p><p>When environmental review is done, WSDOT can commence with the
  activities that lead to a construction contract, including property acquisition,
  final design, construction permits, and solicitation of the bids for construction.
  The timeline for this post-NEPA work is approximately 12-24 months depending on
  which alternative moves forward under the NEPA review.</p><p>WSDOT has more information
  about the next steps for the SR 165 Carbon River/Fairfax Bridge project on the <a
  href=\"https://wsdotblog.blogspot.com/2026/01/next-steps-for-carbon-river-bridge.html\">WSDOT
  Blog</a>.&nbsp;</p><h3>Background</h3><p>The 104-year-old SR 165 Carbon River/Fairfax
  Bridge was&nbsp;<a href=\"/about/news/2025/103-year-old-sr-165-carbon-river-fairfax-bridge-permanently-closed\">permanently
  closed</a> on April 22 after inspections&nbsp;revealed new deterioration of steel
  supports across the bridge. <a href=\"https://www.flickr.com/photos/wsdot/albums/72177720325129239/\">Photos</a>
  show the&nbsp;<a href=\"https://www.flickr.com/photos/wsdot/54459809307/in/album-72177720325129239\">bridge
  support column</a> is bent in two directions and starting to buckle.&nbsp;</p><p>The
  494-foot-long bridge opened to travelers in 1921.&nbsp;In July 2024, the bridge’s
  load rating was reduced to&nbsp;16,000 pounds, or 8 tons.&nbsp;This was the third

The Washington State Department of Transportation (WSDOT) has released the final planning study report for the SR 165 Carbon River/Fairfax Bridge project, which addresses access across the Carbon River Canyon. Following the bridge's closure in April, WSDOT accelerated the $1.5 million study and is now working on the preliminary design and environmental review of two options: replacing the existing bridge or permanently closing it. Approximately $7 million from the 2025-27 Transportation Budget is allocated for this phase.

Survey crews are gathering soil and topographic data to determine suitable locations for a new bridge. WSDOT is seeking right-of-way permits to facilitate soil and rock condition assessments. This data will inform the bridge's design.

The project will undergo a National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) review, evaluating impacts on various environmental factors. Multiple agencies will review WSDOT's findings, with a total review timeline of about 24 months. Public input opportunities will be announced during the NEPA process.

Once the review is completed, WSDOT will proceed with property acquisition, final design, and construction permits, which may take an additional 12-24 months. The SR 165 Carbon River/Fairfax Bridge has been closed since April due to significant structural deterioration.
