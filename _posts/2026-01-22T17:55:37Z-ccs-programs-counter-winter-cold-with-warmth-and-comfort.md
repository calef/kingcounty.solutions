During the cold winter months, Catholic Community Services (CCS) provides crucial support to vulnerable populations in Western Washington. With temperatures dropping, individuals experiencing homelessness and low-income families face increased risks, including hypothermia and food insecurity due to rising heating costs. CCS programs, such as Hope House in Bellingham and Nativity House Shelter in Tacoma, distribute essential items like coats, socks, gloves, and blankets to those in need. Hope House also offers a warm space with beverages and snacks for people to gather and warm up. The Nativity House has benefited from a long-standing clothing drive, receiving over 100 bags of warm clothing. Meanwhile, the Safe Parking Site in Tacoma supplies cold-weather essentials to those living in their cars, and the Housing and Essential Needs program in King County enhances their support bags with hats and gloves. Other community initiatives include the Farmworker Center in Skagit County, which provides winter gear to farmworker families. CCS's efforts, supported by donations and volunteers, aim to ensure that all community members have access to warmth and safety during the harsh winter season.
