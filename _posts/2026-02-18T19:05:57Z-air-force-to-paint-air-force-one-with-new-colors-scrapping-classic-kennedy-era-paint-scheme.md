The U.S. Air Force is set to update the color scheme for its executive fleet, including the next-generation Air Force One aircraft, moving from the classic white and two-tone blue design established during John F. Kennedy's presidency to a new palette of red, white, dark blue, and gold. This decision comes after President Donald Trump's previous proposal for a similar design was rejected due to technical and cost concerns. The new scheme will be applied to a luxury Boeing 747 donated by Qatar in 2025, which is being modified for presidential use, as well as two new Boeing 747-8s. Additionally, four existing C-32 aircraft, a military version of the Boeing 747, will also feature the new colors. An Air Force spokesperson confirmed that the first of these C-32 aircraft has already been painted and is expected to be delivered soon. The earlier plan to change the fleet's colors was scrapped after a thermal study indicated that the darker blue would necessitate further qualification testing by the Federal Aviation Administration. The Air Force has not publicly commented on whether the cost or technical adjustments influenced the new decision.
