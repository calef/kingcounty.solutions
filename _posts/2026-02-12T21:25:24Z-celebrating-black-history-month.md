In celebration of Black History Month, Bellevue LifeSpring is honoring three influential leaders—Shirley Chisholm, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., and Mickey Leland—who made significant contributions to economic justice and access to essential needs for children and families in the U.S. 

Shirley Chisholm (1924-2005) was the first Black woman elected to Congress in 1968, advocating for expanded funding for early childhood education, school nutrition programs, and income supports. She was a strong proponent of federal food assistance, helping to establish the WIC program in 1972.

Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. (1929-1968) is well-known for his role in the Civil Rights Movement but also focused on economic justice in his later years. He initiated the Poor People’s Campaign in 1968 to address systemic poverty, advocating for living wages, decent housing, and federal actions to combat hunger.

Mickey Leland (1944-1989), a Texas congressman, dedicated his career to fighting hunger and was a key voice on food insecurity. He championed the Mickey Leland Childhood Hunger Relief Act, emphasizing that no child should go hungry.

Bellevue LifeSpring reaffirms its commitment to ensuring that every child has the resources necessary to thrive.
