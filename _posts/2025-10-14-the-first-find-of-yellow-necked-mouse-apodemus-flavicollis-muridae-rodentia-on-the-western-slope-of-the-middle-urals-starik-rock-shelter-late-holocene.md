Bone remains of the yellow-necked mouse (Apodemus flavicollis) were found in Holocene deposits at the Starik rock shelter in the Middle Urals. These specimens were collected during excavations in 2008 and are now part of the museum collection at the Institute of Plant and Animal Ecology. The stratigraphic context of the findings dates back 200-300 years. Analysis of the dental morphometrics shows similarities to both modern and Holocene populations of the yellow-necked mouse in the Urals. This specimen represents the first and only documentation of the species in the Middle Urals, offering significant insights into its historical distribution at the eastern edge of its current range.
