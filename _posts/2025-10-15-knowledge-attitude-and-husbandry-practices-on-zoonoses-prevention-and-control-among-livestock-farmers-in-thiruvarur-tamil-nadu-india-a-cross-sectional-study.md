A study conducted in Thiruvarur, Tamil Nadu, India, assessed livestock farmers' knowledge, attitudes, and practices regarding the prevention and control of zoonotic diseases. The research involved 565 farmers and was carried out from October to December 2024, yielding a 95.6% response rate. Findings showed that only 31.7% of farmers had good knowledge, while 55% maintained positive attitudes, and 47.2% practiced good husbandry related to zoonoses. Factors linked to lower risk of effective zoonotic disease management included being a public sector employee, owning land, and using intensive farming systems. Conversely, farmers with livestock shelters located less than 100 meters from their homes faced a higher risk of poor practices. The study concluded that there is a significant gap in awareness and practices among livestock farmers regarding zoonotic diseases. It emphasized the need for targeted educational programs and interventions focused on high-risk groups, particularly those who do not own land or use extensive farming systems. Promoting safer housing practices and enhancing farmers' skills are crucial steps toward improving zoonotic disease prevention and control.
