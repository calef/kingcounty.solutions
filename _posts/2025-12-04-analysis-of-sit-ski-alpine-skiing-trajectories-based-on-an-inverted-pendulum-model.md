A study published in Scientific Reports examines sit-ski alpine skiing trajectories during gate turns using an inverted pendulum model paired with a kinematic testing system. The research involved 11 elite sit-ski athletes and utilized inertial sensors along with drone video analysis. The inertial sensor system demonstrated a static accuracy of 2 degrees and an average deviation of 0.008 meters, while the drone analysis reported a mean relative error of 1.36%. The analysis of 33 gate turns indicated an average skiing distance of 13.61 meters and a mean time of 0.88 seconds. A significant positive correlation was found between single-gate skiing time and both minimum distance and lateral distance. The simulation revealed a center of mass trajectory length of 97.93 meters, which was shorter than actual values, and a simulated time of 6.36 seconds, showing strong consistency with actual results. The findings suggest that improving the balance of turning radius, speed, and distance can decrease skiing time, highlighting the model's potential for tailoring trajectory optimization in sit-ski alpine skiing.
