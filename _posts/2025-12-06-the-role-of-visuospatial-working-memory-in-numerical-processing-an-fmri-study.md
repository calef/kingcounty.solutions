A study examined the relationship between Visual-Spatial Working Memory (VSWM) and number processing in adults using functional MRI (fMRI). Forty student participants were divided into High and Low VSWM groups based on their performance in a VSWM task. During an fMRI scan, participants completed a Number line estimation task. Results showed that the Low VSWM Group had longer reaction times in symbolic numerical processing compared to the High VSWM Group. Neuroanatomical analysis indicated that the High VSWM Group displayed greater activation in the right fronto-parietal regions, while the Low VSWM Group activated the left hemisphere, specifically in parietal, frontal, and temporal areas. Further analysis revealed that activation in the left intraparietal sulcus correlated positively with performance on the Number line Task, highlighting its role in processing symbolic numbers. The findings suggest that individuals with High VSWM efficiently utilize their cognitive resources for symbolic number processing, whereas those with Low VSWM rely more on verbal skills to compensate for their lower spatial abilities.
