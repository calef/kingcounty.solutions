An advance directive is a crucial legal document that outlines an individual's healthcare preferences in case they cannot communicate their wishes due to illness or injury. It consists of two main parts: a healthcare power of attorney, which designates a trusted person to make medical decisions on your behalf, and a living will, which specifies your desires regarding medical treatments such as life support and pain management.

Creating an advance directive proactively ensures that your values guide your care, alleviating emotional stress for your loved ones during difficult times. It is recommended to include preferences about life-prolonging measures, location of care, and organ donation. While advance directives are recognized nationwide, specific requirements may differ by state, and individuals who reside in multiple states should complete the applicable forms for each.

Updating your directive every ten years or after significant life events is important. Additionally, individuals with serious illnesses might consider a POLST (Portable Orders for Life Sustaining Treatment) to provide actionable instructions for emergency responders. After preparing your documents, ensure they are easily accessible to healthcare agents and family members.
