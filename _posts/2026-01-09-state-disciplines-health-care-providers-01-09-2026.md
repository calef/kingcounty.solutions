  \           <div>\n<p><strong>For immediate release</strong>: January 9, 2026  
  (26-005)</p>\n\n<p><strong>Contact</strong>: <a>DOH Communications</a><br>\nPublic
  inquiries: Health Systems Customer Service 360-236-4700</p>\n\n<div>\n<p><strong>OLYMPIA</strong>
  -- The Washington State Department of Health has taken disciplinary actions or withdrawn
  charges against the following health care providers in our state.</p>\n\n<p>The
  department’s Health Systems Quality Assurance Division works with boards, commissions,
  and advisory committees to set licensing standards for more than 80 health care
  professions (e.g., dentists, nursing assistants, counselors).</p>\n\n<p>Information
  about health care providers is on the agency website. Click on “<a>Health Care Provider
  Lookup</a>” under the “Find it Fast” section of the <a>Department of Health website</a>
  (doh.wa.gov).The site includes information about a health care provider’s license
  status, the expiration and renewal date of their credential, disciplinary actions
  and copies of legal documents issued after July 1998. This information is also available
  by calling 360-236-4700. Consumers who think a health care provider acted unprofessionally
  are encouraged to call and report their complaint.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Benton County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health charged registered nursing assistant <strong>Ramon
  Castaneda</strong> (NA60523611) with unprofessional conduct. In November 2024, the
  department received a complaint alleging inappropriate sexual conduct. Castaneda
  failed to respond to the department’s request for information.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Chelan
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In November 2025 the Dental Quality Assurance Commission
  denied <strong>Maria Rosario Arnaga-Rodriguez’s </strong>(D161670226) application
  for a dental assistant credential. In May 2023, Arnaga-Rodriguez was convicted of
  second-degree theft, a class C felony.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Island County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Pharmacy Quality Assurance Commission withdrew its statement of
  charges against pharmacist <strong>Aaron Matthew Syring</strong> (PH00040713).</p>\n\n<h2><strong>King
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health granted <strong>Felix
  Itoto Eshesimua’s </strong>(NA70064150) application for a registered nursing assistant
  credential with conditions.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health
  charged massage therapist <strong>Meijun Guo</strong> (MA60374295) with unprofessional
  conduct. In January and February 2025, Guo allegedly failed to comply with the terms
  and conditions of a July 2023 agreed order, including failing to notify the department
  of changes to her address and employment and failing to respond to the department’s
  requests for information after relocating out of state.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025
  the Examining Board of Psychology and <strong>Steven R. Kubacki</strong> (PY00002316)
  entered an agreed order placing his psychologist credential on probation for at
  least 2.5 years and subject to additional conditions.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025
  the Pharmacy Quality Assurance Commission granted <strong>Kris Daviel Martinez’s</strong>
  (IR70007624) application for a pharmacist intern credential and immediately placed
  the credential on probation. In July 2025, Martinez was charged in Massachusetts
  with felony larceny from a person (equivalent to a class C felony in Washington),
  two counts of assault and battery with a dangerous weapon (equivalent to a class
  B felony in Washington) and assault and battery on a family or household member
  (equivalent to a gross misdemeanor in Washington).</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the
  Department of Health charged home care aide <strong>Kyon J. Saucier </strong>(HM60824302)
  with unprofessional conduct. In December 2023, Saucier allegedly used inappropriate
  physical force toward a child and made threatening and inappropriate statements
  to students. During the department’s investigation, Saucier admitted to physically
  restraining a child.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health terminated
  the probation on <strong>Tianna Marie Stevens’ </strong>(CO61290958) substance use
  disorder professional trainee credential.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Pierce County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health notified <strong>Edward Cheers</strong> (CG61359388)
  of its intent to issue a cease-and-desist order for the unlicensed practice of counseling.
  Beginning in February 2024, Cheers continued to provide therapy services after his
  agency affiliated counselor credential expired.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department
  of Health charged registered nursing assistant <strong>Joswel Renan Lino </strong>(NA61364205)
  with unprofessional conduct. Between April and October 2024, Lino allegedly engaged
  in an inappropriate relationship with a patient. Lino failed to respond to the department’s
  requests for information.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health terminated
  the probation on <strong>Dennis Gregory Savare’s </strong>(CO61364471) substance
  use disorder professional trainee credential.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Snohomish County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health and <a><strong>Kenneth Karlas Kensinger</strong>
  (NC10013240) </a>entered an agreed order reinstating Kensinger’s certified nursing
  assistant credential with conditions.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Spokane County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health terminated the probation on <strong>Zackery
  Allen Fields’ </strong>(CP61636110, CO61346451) substance use disorder professional
  and substance use disorder professional trainee credentials.</p>\n\n<p><a>In October
  2025 the Department of Health reinstated<strong> Mary Eyvonne Green’s</strong> (NC60014915)
  certified nursing assistant credential.</a></p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department
  of Health granted <strong>Jamian Robaire Horner’s</strong> (NA61416434) application
  for a registered nursing assistant credential with conditions.</p>\n\n<p>In December
  2025 the Department of Health granted <strong>Jay Wendlyn Thielen’s</strong> (CO70050489)
  application for a substance use disorder professional trainee credential with conditions
  and placed the credential on probation for at least three years.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Thurston
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health charged substance
  use disorder professional trainee, social worker trainee and social worker<strong>
  Tiffany Page Chhuom </strong>(CO60777461, SC60784501, LW61149320) with unprofessional
  conduct. Beginning in 2021, Chhuom allegedly misrepresented her education and credentials
  and engaged in misleading and retaliatory conduct, including the misuse of Child
  Protective Services and Adult Protective Services reporting.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Whatcom
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In October 2025 the Department of Health charged registered
  nursing assistant <strong>Rachel Openshaw-Tapia</strong> (NA61221174) with unprofessional
  conduct. In January 2025, Openshaw-Tapia allegedly admitted to electronically distributing
  sexually explicit images involving minors. During the investigation, Openshaw-Tapia
  was found to have also recorded protected patient identification information on
  her personal phone.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Whitman County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In December
  2025 the Department of Health charged registered agency affiliated counselor <strong>Deaneal
  Paul McKnight</strong> (CG61238040) with unprofessional conduct. In October 2024,
  McKnight was allegedly terminated from employment following concerns about unprofessional
  conduct and job performance. In 2025, Adult Protective Services investigated McKnight
  in connection with the care of a vulnerable adult. McKnight failed to notify the
  department of his employment termination and failed to respond to the department’s
  requests for information.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Out of State</strong></h2>\n\n<p><strong>Idaho</strong>:
  In December 2025 the Department of Health granted <strong>Laurel Anne Nielsen’s
  </strong>(CG70049981) application for an agency affiliated counselor credential
  with conditions and placed the credential on probation for at least two years.</p>\n</div>\n\n<p><strong>Oregon</strong>:
  In December 2025 the Department of Health and <strong>Dustin Leo Wendelin</strong>
  (NC70000653) entered an agreement granting Wendelin’s application for a certified
  nursing assistant credential. The credential is subject to the terms and conditions
  of the agreement and is on probation until Aug. 12, 2026.</p>\n\n<p><strong>Note
  to Editors</strong>: Health care providers charged with unprofessional conduct have
  20 days to respond to the Department of Health in writing. The case then enters
  the settlement process. If no disciplinary agreement can be reached, the case will
  go to a hearing.</p>\n\n<p>Our <a>website</a> is your source for a healthy dose
  of information. Get updates by following us on <a>social media</a>.</p>\n\n<p>###</p>\n\n<p>                                                                                          <span><span><span>

title: State disciplines health care providers (01-09-2026)
topic_titles:
- Health Care
---

The Washington State Department of Health announced disciplinary actions against several health care providers as of January 9, 2026. The department's Health Systems Quality Assurance Division oversees licensing for over 80 health professions. Complaints against providers include unprofessional conduct, such as inappropriate relationships and failure to comply with regulations.

In Benton County, registered nursing assistant Ramon Castaneda was charged with unprofessional conduct after allegations of inappropriate sexual conduct. In Chelan County, Maria Rosario Arnaga-Rodriguez had her dental assistant application denied due to a felony conviction. King County saw charges against massage therapist Meijun Guo for failing to adhere to previous agreements, while psychologist Steven R. Kubacki received probation.

Other actions included the termination of probation for several professionals and notifications of cease-and-desist orders for unlicensed practice. In Snohomish County, certified nursing assistant Kenneth Karlas Kensinger had his credential reinstated under conditions.

Providers facing charges have 20 days to respond in writing, after which cases may go to a hearing if a settlement cannot be reached. For more information, the public can access the Health Care Provider Lookup on the department's website or call their customer service.
