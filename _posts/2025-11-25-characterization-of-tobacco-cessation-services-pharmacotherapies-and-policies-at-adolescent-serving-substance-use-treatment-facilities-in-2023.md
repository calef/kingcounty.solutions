A study examined tobacco cessation resources available in adolescent substance use treatment facilities across the United States. Researchers analyzed data from the 2023 National Substance Use and Mental Health Services Survey, comparing facilities that exclusively serve adolescents (ages 11-21) with those that serve both adolescents and adults. 

Most adolescent-only facilities provided tobacco use screening (82.6%) and education or counseling for cessation (71.9%), similar to adult/adolescent facilities. However, adolescent-only facilities were more likely to prohibit smoking (84.7% vs. 33.1%) and vaping (87.7% vs. 41.9%). Despite these restrictions, only 20.7% of adolescent-only facilities offered tobacco cessation pharmacotherapies, such as nicotine replacement therapy, compared to 45.5% in adult/adolescent facilities. 

These findings suggest that while adolescent-only treatment facilities focus on education and policy against tobacco use, they fall short in providing pharmacological support for tobacco cessation, indicating a missed opportunity to effectively address tobacco use in adolescents with substance use disorders.
