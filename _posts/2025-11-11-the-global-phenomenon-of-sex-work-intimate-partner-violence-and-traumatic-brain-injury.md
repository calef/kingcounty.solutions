Sex work is predominantly a female profession, with 60% to 80% of workers identifying as women. A significant concern within this group is the high rate of traumatic brain injuries (TBI) related to intimate partner violence (IPV). Research indicates that around 80% of female sex workers have experienced IPV, with 95% reporting TBIs, often from being struck or having their heads slammed. In contrast, only 10% of IPV survivors in the general population report TBIs. Despite the alarming prevalence, there is limited research on the intersection of IPV and TBI among sex workers, with most studies focusing on sexually transmitted diseases and law enforcement issues. This commentary highlights the gap in research regarding TBI risks for female sex workers, advocating for recognition of this group as high-risk for TBI. It draws on global examples from Africa, Asia, North America, and Oceania to discuss occupational hazards and suggests using harm reduction strategies to improve care and intervention for these vulnerable individuals. The authors call for more attention from the TBI research community to address this critical issue in an underrepresented population.
