A significant ruling from Washington's Court of Appeals has established new requirements for how trial courts manage gun surrender in domestic violence cases. The case, Dela Llana v. Holiwell, involved Grenadinah Dela Llana seeking a domestic violence protection order against Darrion Holiwell, a former sheriff’s deputy. The court mandated Holiwell to surrender his firearms, but he claimed to have none, despite records showing he owned 70 weapons. Over nearly two years and 20 hearings, the court found Holiwell repeatedly contemptuous, ultimately issuing a $100,000 fine, which proved ineffective due to his claimed indigence.

The Court of Appeals reversed the lower court's leniency, emphasizing that trial courts must verify compliance with weapon surrender orders and that the burden of proof lies with the abuser. This ruling mandates swift compliance to safeguard survivors, as access to firearms significantly increases the risk of fatal outcomes in domestic violence situations. The decision is now binding statewide, ensuring that trial courts can employ all necessary measures, including indefinite jail time, to ensure that abusers relinquish their firearms. This landmark victory underscores the importance of effective gun surrender policies in protecting domestic violence survivors.
