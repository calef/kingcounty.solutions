  <h2>Virtual support leads to lasting results</h2>



  <p>Diana Calero’s 16 years of service didn’t end when she took off her Navy uniform. She took on a new role as a Customs and Border Protection officer at Miami International Airport. Even with exhausting shifts, Calero was determined to take care of her health. She wanted to lose the weight she had gained after service.</p>



  <p>But like so many Veterans balancing their personal lives with demanding jobs, Calero found it tough to make real, lasting progress. “I was always working out, but the results just weren’t there,” she shared.</p>



  <p>Wanting a clear starting place, she visited the Women’s Health Clinic at the <a>Miami VA Healthcare System</a> to check her body mass index. She walked out with something much more valuable: a referral to the Women’s Passport to Wellness program and a wellness coach who would help change her outlook.</p>



  <p>The Women’s Passport to Wellness program connects women Veterans with personalized coaching, resources and support—including convenient telehealth options—so they can achieve their health and wellness goals on their own schedule.</p>



  <h2>Small tweaks, big shifts</h2>



  <p>Meeting wellness coach Otmara Soberanes marked a turning point.</p>



  <p>“Otmara explained everything,” Calero said. “This wasn’t just about the scale; it was about me. We set goals for my lifestyle, what felt achievable and what mattered to me.”</p>



  <p>Sessions were mostly held virtually through VA Video Connect, VA’s secure videoconferencing app. The video sessions were a huge benefit, given Calero’s 4 a.m. to noon work schedule and Miami’s legendary traffic. “I never felt disconnected. Even on video, the support was there. And the flexibility fit my life.”</p>



  <p>Calero learned that progress meant steady, sustainable changes and nothing extreme or unrealistic.</p>



  <p>“Before, I thought it was all about intense workouts. But Otmara helped me focus on things like trading out some of my favorite foods for healthier options, like cutting back on dairy or planning how to eat out. She gave me step challenges, hydration reminders and sometimes a fun challenge for weeks when I was traveling or not feeling my best.”</p>



  <p>It worked. Calero lost more than 20 pounds, and her BMI went from 28.4 (overweight) to 24.7 (healthy weight). More importantly, she has kept the weight off.</p>



  <p>“I used to worry that one setback would erase everything. But now I know how to bounce back and not stress,” she said.</p>



  <h2>Confidence on the rise</h2>



  <p>The biggest shift for Calero? How she felt.</p>



  <p>“Accomplishing these goals made me realize what I’m capable of,” Calero said. “Even if the scale doesn’t move much sometimes, the changes stick. Slow progress is still progress.”</p>



  <p>She encourages other Veterans to be patient with themselves and to be open to support along the way. According to Calero, the program is most effective when participants put in consistent effort. Her wellness coach encouraged her through plateaus and celebrated every small victory—and that support helped. Calero believes persistence is key, even if changes happen gradually.</p>



  <p>Ready to make a long-term change? Ask your VA provider about telehealth or wellness resources that can fit your schedule and your goals—or visit <a>VA Telehealth Services</a> to learn more.</p>

Diana Calero, a former Navy service member now working as a Customs and Border Protection officer, sought to improve her health after gaining weight post-service. Struggling to see results from her workouts, she visited the Women’s Health Clinic at the Miami VA Healthcare System, where she received a referral to the Women’s Passport to Wellness program and a wellness coach. This program connects women Veterans with personalized coaching, resources, and the convenience of telehealth options.

Calero began working with wellness coach Otmara Soberanes, who shifted her focus from intense workouts to sustainable lifestyle changes. They held sessions mainly through VA Video Connect, allowing Calero to fit them into her busy schedule. By making small adjustments, such as healthier food choices and hydration reminders, Calero lost over 20 pounds and reduced her BMI from 28.4 to 24.7, maintaining her weight loss long-term.

Calero emphasized the importance of patience and consistent effort, stating that even gradual progress is still progress. She encourages other Veterans to seek support and consider wellness resources available through the VA to help achieve their health goals.
