In April 2025, the 104-year-old SR 165 Carbon River/Fairfax Bridge was closed due to critical structural issues. A planning study has since been completed to explore long-term solutions for crossing the Carbon River. This study evaluated multiple options, including replacing the bridge at its current site, rerouting the highway, or permanently closing the bridge. Public feedback favored restoring access to Mount Rainier National Park and minimizing costs.

Two alternatives have advanced to the design and environmental review phase: replacing the bridge and permanently closing it. A $7 million budget has been allocated for this next phase, which will include gathering geotechnical information and obtaining necessary environmental clearances. The area is known for its sensitive wildlife, requiring careful planning to protect habitats.

A temporary bypass route has been established for residents affected by the bridge closure, but it remains privately owned and gated. Funding challenges have hindered previous maintenance efforts on aging bridges statewide, highlighting the need for better infrastructure support. A 24-month timeline has been set for the environmental review process, with opportunities for public input during this period.
