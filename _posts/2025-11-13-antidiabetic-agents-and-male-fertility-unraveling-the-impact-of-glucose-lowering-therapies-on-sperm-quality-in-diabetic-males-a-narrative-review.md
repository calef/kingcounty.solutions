  **ABSTRACT**

  BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVE: Diabetes mellitus imposes a substantial burden on male fertility, adversely affecting sperm quality through structural damage in the reproductive tract, endocrine disruption, and dysregulation of cellular metabolism. While glucose-lowering agents help achieve glycemic control, they may also ameliorate diabetes-induced impairment of sperm quality. However, these drugs exert heterogeneous effects on male reproduction via divergent mechanisms, with outcomes often varying between _in vivo_ and _in vitro_ settings. This review aims to systematically evaluate the impacts of different antihyperglycemic therapies on semen quality and elucidate their mechanistic bases, to guide therapy selection for improving fertility in diabetic men.

  METHODS: A comprehensive search of the PubMed and Embase databases was conducted, covering the period from their inception to 2025.

  KEY CONTENT AND FINDINGS: Insulin enhances sperm DNA integrity via Hsp70-2/TP-1/TP-2 upregulation and acts as a cryoprotectant. Metformin activates AMPK-mediated autophagy and synergizes with zinc to preserve fertility. SGLT-2 inhibitors suppress testicular apoptosis via Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant pathways. Critically, sulfonylureas improve _in vivo_ sperm quality but exert spermicidal effects in vitro by disrupting calcium homeostasis.

  CONCLUSIONS: Current evidence supports the potential of certain antihyperglycemic agents in mitigating diabetes-related male infertility, though drug-specific and context-dependent effects must be considered. Further phenotype-targeted studies are needed to optimize clinical treatment strategies.

Diabetes mellitus significantly impacts male fertility by damaging sperm quality through structural harm to the reproductive tract, endocrine disruption, and metabolic dysregulation. While glucose-lowering medications can help manage blood sugar levels, their effects on sperm quality vary widely. A systematic review evaluates the impact of various antihyperglycemic therapies on semen quality and aims to clarify the mechanisms involved. 

Insulin is noted to improve sperm DNA integrity and serve as a cryoprotectant. Metformin is found to activate autophagy while working alongside zinc to maintain fertility. SGLT-2 inhibitors protect against testicular apoptosis through antioxidant pathways, whereas sulfonylureas enhance sperm quality in vivo but may have adverse effects in vitro by affecting calcium levels. 

The findings suggest that some antihyperglycemic agents may alleviate diabetes-related male infertility, though their effects are drug-specific and context-dependent. The study calls for further phenotype-targeted research to refine treatment strategies for diabetic men facing fertility challenges.
