Veterans facing homelessness encounter significant obstacles in accessing traditional U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) care, even when temporarily sheltered on VA grounds. To address this issue, an encampment medicine team was established at the West Los Angeles VA. This team aimed to provide primary care, psychiatry, and harm reduction services within a community of tiny shelters. Utilizing street outreach principles and a focus on continuous quality improvement, the team embedded their services around the shelters and worked closely with peer support specialists and social workers. They adapted their care based on real-time feedback from a veteran-led engagement committee. Over a period of 15 months, the team conducted 923 visits with 181 veterans, initiated long-acting antipsychotic treatments, increased naloxone distribution, administered vaccines, assisted with housing transitions, and achieved seven hepatitis C cures. By integrating continuous quality improvement into a low-barrier, person-centered care model, the team fostered trust and enhanced health engagement among veterans. This innovative approach serves as a potential model for effectively reaching high-need veterans in emergency housing situations.
