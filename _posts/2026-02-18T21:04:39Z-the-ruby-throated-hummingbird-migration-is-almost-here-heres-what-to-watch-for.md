The ruby-throated hummingbird migration begins in early March as these birds travel from Central America and Mexico to southern Canada. This remarkable journey, often involving a nonstop flight of up to 500 miles across the Gulf of Mexico, marks the onset of spring for birdwatchers in the U.S., particularly from Texas to Florida. Males typically arrive first to scout feeding territories rich in early-blooming flowers and backyard feeders.

From March to May, the migration extends northward as the birds follow the blooming of nectar-producing plants, moving in sync with warming temperatures. By late spring, they can be found throughout the eastern U.S. and into southern Canada. The migration is primarily triggered by increasing daylight, which stimulates hormonal changes, while food availability plays a crucial role in timing their journey.

Birdwatchers are encouraged to look for these small, fast-moving birds around early-blooming flowers and nectar feeders, particularly in the early morning and late afternoon when they feed most actively. Planting native tubular flowers and setting up feeders can enhance the chances of spotting them. Their distinctive high-pitched sounds and rapidly beating wings are also indicators of their presence.
