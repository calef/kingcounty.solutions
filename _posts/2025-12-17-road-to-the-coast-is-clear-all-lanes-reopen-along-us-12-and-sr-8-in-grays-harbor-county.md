  class=\"field field--name-body field--type-text-with-summary field--label-hidden
  field--item\"><h2>Multi-site fish passage project wraps up between Olympia and Aberdeen</h2><p>ELMA
  – There’s good news to share for people who use US 12 and State Route 8 in Grays
  Harbor County. All lanes are now open following the completion of a fish passage
  project that covered five work zones near McCleary, Elma and Montesano.</p><p>On
  Wednesday, Dec. 17, contractor crews working for the Washington State Department
  of Transportation reopened lanes at two work zones near Elma. The highway was reduced
  to one lane in each direction for <a href=\"https://www.flickr.com/photos/wsdot/albums/72177720313515824\">fish
  passage work</a> that started in summer 2023. Three other locations in McCleary
  and Montesano reopened earlier this year.</p><p>WSDOT thanks travelers for their
  continued patience while crews finish landscaping and final touches. People will
  see occasional daytime lane closures during this work.</p><h3>What was done</h3><p>To
  keep people moving during the <a href=\"/construction-planning/search-projects/us-12-sr-8-grays-harbor-county-fish-passage-barriers-remove-fish-barriers\">estimated
  $109 million project</a>, crews built a temporary lane across the median at each
  location. This allowed crews to close one side of the highway at a time for construction
  while the other side was reconfigured to handle both directions of traffic.</p><p>During
  the lane closures, crews replaced outdated culverts with nine bridges and one larger
  culvert under US 12 and SR 8. The new bridges and culvert not only now allow fish
  to <a href=\"https://wsdotblog.blogspot.com/2025/05/baby-salmon-doo-doo-doo-doo-doo-doo-and.html\">swim
  freely</a> under the highway, they are built to modern earthquake standards and
  help prevent flooding on the roadway. This makes the highway <a href=\"https://wsdotblog.blogspot.com/2024/08/grays-harbor-county-fish-barrier-removal.html\">more
  resilient</a>.</p><h3>About fish passage projects</h3><p>State highways cross over
  rivers and streams in thousands of locations throughout Washington. Some crossings
  can impede fish migration. Since 1991, WSDOT has worked to <a href=\"/construction-planning/protecting-environment/fish-passage/improving-fish-passage\">improve
  fish passage</a> and foster healthy waterways by removing barriers under the highway.
  In addition to WSDOT’s ongoing work to correct statewide barriers, a 2013 federal
  court injunction requires the state to correct barrier culverts to salmon and steelhead

All lanes are now open on US 12 and State Route 8 in Grays Harbor County following the completion of a multi-site fish passage project. The Washington State Department of Transportation announced that contractor crews reopened lanes at two work zones near Elma on Dec. 17, after the highway had been reduced to one lane in each direction since summer 2023. Three other locations in McCleary and Montesano also reopened earlier this year.

The $109 million project involved replacing outdated culverts with nine bridges and one larger culvert, allowing fish to swim freely under the highway. The new structures meet modern earthquake standards and help prevent flooding, enhancing the roadway's resilience. To minimize disruptions, crews built temporary lanes across the median, allowing for construction while maintaining traffic flow.

WSDOT appreciates travelers' patience as crews finish landscaping and final touches, with occasional daytime lane closures expected. Since 1991, WSDOT has worked to improve fish passage across state highways to support healthy waterways and comply with federal requirements to remove barriers to salmon and steelhead in western Washington.
