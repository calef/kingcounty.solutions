A study evaluated the effectiveness of suture fixation for treating simple olecranon fractures, suggesting it results in low complication and reoperation rates, along with favorable functional outcomes compared to the traditional tension band wiring method. Between 2017 and 2024, 34 patients with olecranon fractures underwent suture fixation, including various fracture types. The mean follow-up period was 31.4 months. 

Results showed only one patient needed revision due to fixation failure, while another had a refracture treated with plate fixation. Two patients experienced superficial infections, managed with oral antibiotics. The average radiographic healing time was 1.48 months. Functional outcomes included a mean extension of 6.76 degrees, flexion of 139.11 degrees, and nearly full range of motion in pronation and supination. The mean scores on the quick Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand, and the Mayo Elbow Performance Score were 3.15 and 95.3, respectively, with a low average pain rating of 1.2.

Overall, suture fixation is a reliable and effective alternative for treating simple olecranon fractures, presenting lower complication rates than previously documented techniques.
