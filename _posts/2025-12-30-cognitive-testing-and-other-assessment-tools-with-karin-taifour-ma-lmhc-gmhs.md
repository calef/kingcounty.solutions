  <p>This workshop is a A thorough examination of mental status along with cognitive testing and functional evaluations are key parts of a comprehensive geriatric assessment. Test scores are objective data that complement the subjective observations and impressions.</p>



  <p>Numerous cognitive testing tools and other assessment instruments are used in clinical and social services contexts throughout the United States and internationally. However, there is not standard training across medical or graduate education programs on the use and administration of various tools, and certification of training or proficiency is not typically required or even available.  In most settings, clinicians have had some continuing education or self-study with little to no formal training, practical experience, or feedback from experts.  </p>



  <p>This workshop will review the various cognitive testing tools and other assessment instruments that may be used in the process of a comprehensive geriatric assessment.</p>



  <p>In this workshop you will learn to:</p>



  <ul>
  <li>Explain the role of cognitive testing and other assessment tools in process of client assessments.</li>



  <li>Know how to determine what type of tools/tests to use in different situations.</li>



  <li>Compare types of screening tools, cognitive tests, informant tools, mood/anxiety scales, functional assessments, and decision-related interview tools.</li>



  <li> Identify ethical/systemic/logistical factors as well as social/cultural factors to consider in     testing/assessment.</li>



  <li> Identify limitations and compensatory/support strategies in the testing process.</li>



  <li>Know how to explain the testing process, scores, and interpretation in assessment documentation.</li>
  </ul>



  <h2>About the Instructor</h2>



  <p>Karin Taifour, MA LMHC GMHS, holds a masters is Applied Behavioral Sciences from Bastyr University and a certificate in geriatric mental health from University of Washington. Karin has been a practicing clinician and consultant for over 20 years, in private practice since 2017. A qualified clinical supervisor, Karin also has taught graduate level courses and provided professional training and continuing education, as well as public/community presentations. Karin has extensive experience in assessing decision making capacity in older adults and has testified at multiple criminal trials, guardianship proceedings, and vulnerable adult protection order hearings, and administrative hearings.<br>She has written articles and presented at conferences and for organizations such as the Elder Abuse Council of Seattle and King County, the Washington State Bar Association, and the King County Bar Association, the state guardianship certification program, the Washington State Elder Abuse Conference, and others.<br>She is recognized for her expertise and experience in assessing decisional capacity, cognitive functioning, and care needs, and estimates she has assessed over 1000 older adults in her career, with over 150 decisional capacity assessments in the past 8 years.<br>As one of the few clinicians (if not the only one) offering decisional capacity evaluation services in the greater Seattle area, Karin is eager to share this rich area of practice with other professionals to both build understanding of what decisional capacity is and what is involved in assessing it. The demand for these services is growing exponentially, and she looks forward to other clinicians to start working in this area for collaboration and mutual support.</p>

A workshop on cognitive testing and assessment tools for geriatric evaluations will be held on February 4, 2026, from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. PDT. The session, led by Karin Taifour, MA LMHC GMHS, will cover the importance of mental status exams and cognitive testing in comprehensive assessments for older adults. Despite the widespread use of various assessment tools, there is a lack of standard training in their administration among clinicians.

Participants will learn about different cognitive testing tools, how to select appropriate instruments for various situations, and the ethical and cultural considerations in testing. The workshop will also address the limitations of these tools and the interpretation of results in documentation.

Karin Taifour, a seasoned clinician with over 20 years of experience, specializes in assessing decision-making capacity in older adults and has conducted numerous assessments throughout her career. The program will be conducted via Zoom, with a fee of $199 plus tax. Discounts and scholarships are available, and all service providers are encouraged to attend.
