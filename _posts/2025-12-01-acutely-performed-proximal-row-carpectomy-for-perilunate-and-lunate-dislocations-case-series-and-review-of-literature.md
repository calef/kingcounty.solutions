A study evaluated the effectiveness of acute proximal row carpectomy (PRC) as a treatment for perilunate dislocations (PLDs) and perilunate fracture-dislocations (PLFDs). Conducted between 2010 and 2022, the research involved a retrospective chart review of 14 adult patients treated with PRC within 21 days of injury. Most injuries were caused by motor vehicle collisions (57%) and falls (43%). The average time from injury to surgery was 5.6 days, and patients were followed up an average of 68 months post-surgery. The findings showed favorable return-to-work rates, with 35.7% returning without restrictions and another 35.7% with restrictions. Patient-reported outcomes indicated low pain levels (VAS score of 1.31) and positive functional results, with Quick Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder, and Hand scores averaging 20.5 and Patient-Rated Wrist Evaluations averaging 24.5. The study underscores PRC as a viable option for managing PLDs and PLFDs, suggesting it leads to good functional outcomes and low complication rates, addressing the need for more research in this area.
