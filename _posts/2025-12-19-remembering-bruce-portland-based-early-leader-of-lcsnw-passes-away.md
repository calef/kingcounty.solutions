  Bruce Strade (right) and Salah Ansary at LCSNW’s 2024 Faith Leaders breakfast in
  Portland.</em></p>\n<p>Friends and former colleagues are mourning the loss of a
  long-time leader in the Lutheran Community Services Northwest family.</p>\n<p>Bruce
  Strade, 85, died on Dec. 2. He was a key figure in our Oregon operations in the
  1980s and ‘90s. He later had a large role in the 2001 merger that brought three
  states under the LCSNW umbrella. He’s also remembered for many charitable ventures
  in the Portland area after retiring from LCSNW in 2004.</p>\n<p>Salah Ansary, LCSNW’s
  Senior Director of Advocacy &amp; Government Affairs, said Bruce’s big heart and
  deep desire to reunite separated refugee families continued until the end of his
  life.</p>\n<p>“A few weeks before Bruce passed away, he called to tell me about
  an Afghan family (single mother with 3 children), whom he had helped since 2022,”
  Salah said. “The husband’s case was scheduled for an interview in Islamabad. This
  news made Bruce so happy!”</p>\n<p>Salah fondly recalls a trip that he and Bruce
  took to Russia and Ukraine in 1999. They observed the processing of refugees from
  Eastern Europe and visited with refugees bound for the Northwest.</p>\n<p>“Bruce
  was a compassionate individual; he cared for the needy and vulnerable,” Salah said.</p>\n<p>Ordained
  as a Lutheran pastor in 1966, Bruce settled his young family in Portland – where
  he’d met his wife, Kathy – in the 1970s, according to his <a>published obituary</a>.
  He joined Lutheran Family Service of Oregon as a part-time family therapist while
  also serving as Dean of Students at Concordia College.</p>\n<p>In 1980, Bruce was
  named Director of the Washington County office of Lutheran Family Service of Oregon
  and Southwest Washington. In 1994, he was elevated to LFS Executive Director. Under
  his leadership, LFS was recognized as the largest Lutheran refugee resettlement
  agency in the U.S.</p>\n<p>He remained in that position until 2001, when the two
  organizations representing the three-state region came together. <u>Lutheran Family
  Service of Oregon and Southwest Washington</u> merged with <u>Lutheran Social Services
  of Washington and Idaho</u>. Roberta Nestaas, who led LSSWI at the time, was named
  CEO/President of the merged organization; Bruce was named Chief Operating Officer.</p>\n<p>He
  also enjoyed using an alternate title: Chief Officer of Organizational Delight.</p>\n<p>“His
  sense of humor was legendary,” said Boots Winterstein, who served under Bruce when
  she was Director of the North Puget Sound Area of the newly merged LCSNW.</p>\n<p>The
  merger was tricky, Boots recalled – not just the legal part, “but also merging the
  quite different cultures of the two agencies.” She credits Bruce and Roberta for
  working diligently to do that.</p>\n<p></p>\n<div>\n<img src=\"https://lcsnw.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Roberta-and-Bruce-300x164.jpg\"><p>Roberta
  Nestaas and Bruce Strade were the first executives at the newly merged LCSNW in
  2001.</p>\n</div>\n<p>Nearly 25 years later, CEO David Duea says LCSNW is in a strong
  position to serve Northwest communities, thanks in part to the groundwork laid by
  those early leaders.</p>\n<p>“Many of us are saddened by the loss of Bruce in 2025
  and Roberta in 2024, but I was blessed by their guidance and deep history with the
  agency,” David said. “I will never take for granted the seeds they planted for LCSNW’s
  mission of Health, Justice, and Hope.”</p>\n<p>A memorial service for Bruce Strade
  is set for Jan. 10 at 11am at Zion Lutheran Church in Portland.</p>\n</div>\n\t\t\t</div>\n\t\t\t</div>\n<div>\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t<div>\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t<span><img

Bruce Strade, a prominent leader in Lutheran Community Services Northwest (LCSNW), passed away on Dec. 2 at the age of 85. Strade was instrumental in the organization’s Oregon operations during the 1980s and 1990s and played a significant role in the 2001 merger that unified three states under LCSNW. After retiring in 2004, he continued his charitable work in the Portland area. Salah Ansary, LCSNW’s Senior Director of Advocacy & Government Affairs, remembered Strade for his compassion and commitment to helping refugee families, noting that he was still actively involved in supporting an Afghan family weeks before his death. Strade was ordained as a Lutheran pastor in 1966 and began working with Lutheran Family Service of Oregon as a part-time family therapist. He became the Executive Director in 1994, leading the agency to become the largest Lutheran refugee resettlement organization in the U.S. Following the merger, he served as Chief Operating Officer and was known for his sense of humor. A memorial service is scheduled for Jan. 10 at Zion Lutheran Church in Portland.
