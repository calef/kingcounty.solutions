  <h2>From pain to purpose through digital health tools</h2>



  <p>After serving in the Army, one Veteran’s personal challenges made his transition to civilian life more difficult than many, including undergoing multiple back surgeries and experiencing depression after losing both his father and son in the same year.</p>



  <p>“I felt like I had lost purpose, meaning, and direction,” he said. “When I first retired from the military, I really didn’t want anything to do with it. I associated VA with the military and didn’t participate.”</p>



  <p>It wasn’t until years later that he sought help.</p>



  <p>Today, he goes by his Buddhist name, Peaceful Joy, and credits VA’s virtual care resources with helping him transform both his health and his outlook on life.</p>



  <h2>Finding help through virtual care</h2>



  <p>When Joy connected with VA, he discovered a new path forward through digital health tools, including <a>VA mobile apps</a>.</p>



  <p>Through <a>VA’s Mindfulness Coach app</a>, he discovered the power of meditation and was able to rebuild his emotional well-being.</p>



  <p>“Meditation gave me purpose and direction all over again,” he said. “That app gave me the mechanisms to ensure I’m on track and I continue to stay on track. I absolutely am amazed.”</p>



  <p>Joy has since taken what he’s learned and is now teaching other Veterans both in person and virtually by leading mediation groups.</p>



  <p>“I started doing a meditation group for our senior center,” he explained. “It snowballed into much more and gave me a whole new purpose in my life.”</p>



  <h2>A message to other Veterans</h2>



  <p>Joy now encourages all Veterans to seek out the help and resources provided by VA to help find similar success.</p>



  <p>“All Veterans should be aware of the benefits available to them,” he said. “Take a look. They’re free. They’re for you. And they will help you in your life.”</p>



  <p>To learn more about VA’s virtual care resources, like <a>VA mobile apps</a>, visit the <a>Office of Connected Care website</a>.</p>



  <p>Many Veterans find apps like Mindfulness Coach helpful, but they are not a replacement for mental health treatment. For information on how to access VA mental health services please visit <a>VA mental health services</a>.</p>



  <p>To reach the <a>Veterans Crisis Line</a>, <a>Dial 988 then Press 1</a>, <a>chat online</a> at VeteransCrisisLine.net/Chat, or text <a>838255</a>. </p>

A Veteran who faced significant challenges after leaving the Army, including multiple back surgeries and severe depression from personal losses, found purpose and healing through the VA’s digital health tools. Initially resistant to VA services, he eventually sought help and discovered the Mindfulness Coach app, which helped him explore meditation and rebuild his emotional well-being. He now identifies as Peaceful Joy and credits the app with restoring his sense of direction in life. Inspired by his transformation, Joy began leading meditation groups for other Veterans, fostering a sense of community and purpose. He encourages all Veterans to utilize VA resources, emphasizing that these tools are free and can provide meaningful support. Joy’s journey highlights the positive impact of virtual care in addressing mental health challenges for Veterans. While the Mindfulness Coach app is beneficial, he notes it should not replace professional mental health treatment. For assistance, Veterans can access various VA mental health services or reach out to the Veterans Crisis Line.
