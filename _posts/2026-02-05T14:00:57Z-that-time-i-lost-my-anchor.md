Pam Sanderson Lyon, a resident of Durham, North Carolina, reflects on her struggles with anxiety and self-image, particularly during her time in stand-up comedy. Initially, she found purpose in her comedic mission to connect with and uplift her audience. However, as she became increasingly introspective, her anxiety intensified, leading her to lose sight of her goals and her voice. This inward focus contributed to a negative self-perception, exacerbated by the pressures of performing publicly.

Lyon experienced a decline in her mental health, prompting her to take a break from comedy and prioritize self-care. During this time, she made a conscious effort to eat regular meals, combat negative thoughts, and reconnect with her identity beyond her appearance or performance. She found solace in her job at the local library, where she began to feel a sense of community and support.

Through this journey, Lyon acknowledges the importance of external connections and self-acceptance, noting that recovery is an ongoing process. She emphasizes the value of engaging with loved ones and the world around her, as well as the potential for future creative endeavors like writing a book.
