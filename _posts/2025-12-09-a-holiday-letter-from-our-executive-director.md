  <p>Dear Supporter,</p>



  <p>This holiday season, patients in your community need your help.</p>



  <p>For thousands of individuals and families, transportation remains a barrier to essential medical care. <strong>But with your support,</strong><strong>we can continue to serve those who need us most </strong>by making the gift of flight possible for people who count on Angel Flight West.</p>



  <p>Meet our passenger, eight-year-old Eli. At just two years old, he was diagnosed with intractable epilepsy — a condition that makes it difficult to control his seizures. Eli and his family live in Silt, Colorado, nearly 650 miles from his treatment at Phoenix Children’s Hospital.</p>



  <p>Reaching his neurologist meant frequent and exhausting drives across state lines, but Angel Flight West (AFW) has changed that. Together, <strong>we’ve saved Eli’s family time, money, and the burden of a 20-hour round trip to reach his important appointments.</strong> “AFW always finds a way to get us to Phoenix Children’s and back home,” says Eli’s mom Savannah. “The volunteer pilots are wonderful. When Eli doesn’t feel his best, he can relax in their planes.”</p>



  <p><strong>Eli’s story is one of thousands made possible through AFW’s network of supporters and volunteers.</strong></p>



  <p>Your generosity helps ensure that every patient — from children like Eli to adults fighting serious illness — can reach the care they need, free of charge.</p>



  <p><strong>Our Board of Directors added $20,000 to their matching gift! Give by December 23, and your tax-deductible gift will be DOUBLED — at no extra cost to you.</strong></p>



  <p>Thank you for believing in Angel Flight West’s mission and for helping us give hope to so many. From our board, staff, and volunteers, we wish you a happy holiday season.</p>



  <p>With gratitude,</p>



  <p>Mary Hunter<br>Executive Director<br>Angel Flight West</p>

This holiday season, Angel Flight West (AFW) highlights the urgent need for support to help patients access essential medical care. Many individuals and families face transportation challenges, particularly for long distances. A notable example is eight-year-old Eli, who suffers from intractable epilepsy and lives in Silt, Colorado, nearly 650 miles from Phoenix Children’s Hospital. His family previously endured exhausting 20-hour round trips for treatment, but AFW has alleviated this burden, allowing them to reach appointments more easily. Eli's mother, Savannah, praises the volunteer pilots for their kindness and support, which makes travel less stressful for her son. AFW serves thousands of patients, ensuring they can access necessary care at no cost. To further encourage donations, the Board of Directors has contributed an additional $20,000 to a matching gift campaign. Donations made by December 23 will be doubled, enhancing the impact on those in need. Mary Hunter, Executive Director of AFW, expresses gratitude for the support and wishes everyone a happy holiday season.
