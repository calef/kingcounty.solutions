feed_content: "<div>\n\n    \n    <p><img src=\"https://www.dav.org/wp-content/uploads/RecruitMember_Orlando.jpg\">Your
  mission is simple: Sign up just one fully paid new DAV member, and you’ll instantly
  be entered to win the ultimate prize package for the 2026 DAV and Auxiliary National
  Convention in Orlando, Florida. Think sunshine, community and $2,500 in spending
  money—hotel room included. Stop dreaming about a vacation and start recruiting your
  way to one.</p>\n<p>The rules of the contest: Recruit one new paid member between
  now and June 30, 2026, and be automatically entered in the contest. There’s only
  one entry per person, no matter how many new members you recruit. After June 30,
  we’ll hold a drawing at DAV National Headquarters to pick the winner.</p>\n<p>“One
  million members is more than a number—it’s a nonnegotiable floor for our legislative
  strength,” said National Adjutant Barry Jesinoski. “It’s what commands attention
  on Capitol Hill. Let’s strip the recruitment goal down to its simplest form: Find
  one. If every current nonrecruiter brought in just one new member, we don’t just
  secure our advocacy; we exponentially multiply it. Don’t worry about the total—worry
  about the veteran you can personally empower today.”</p>\n<p>And when you recruit
  more than one new member, besides being entered in the Recruiter Rally, you’ll receive
  credits to purchase items from the <a>DAV Store</a> and other possible awards!</p>\n<p><strong>DAV
  super recruiter gives recruiting pro tips</strong></p>\n<p>Recruiting members isn’t
  easy for everyone. Besides the potential for rejection, selling anything is challenging
  for some people because it involves connecting with the “buyer.” But according to
  one DAV “super recruiter,” empathy is your superpower when selling a DAV membership.</p>\n<p>“There
  might be one issue you’re focused on, that you’re passionate about—share that part
  of your passion,” said Shannon Sander, a service officer for the Department of New
  York who recruited more than 150 members last year. “Let people know what DAV is
  fighting for that affects you and your family. Let them know what it was that made
  you join DAV.”</p>\n<p>One of the hardest parts of selling something is the potential
  rejection. People, in general, don’t like hearing “no” because it feels like a limitation
  on their desires or plans. But not Sander.</p>\n<p>“I take ‘no’ as a maybe,” she
  said. “Because when I give them the information and my reason for joining DAV, the
  no right there turns into a maybe. Maybe they need to go home and talk to their
  spouse. Maybe they’re too embarrassed to say they don’t have a job right now. I
  never look at that no as a solid no to membership.”</p>\n<p>The final tip from Sander
  involves money, or rather, how to communicate the cost of joining.</p>\n<p>“Don’t
  pressure people to pay all at once,” she said. “Often, disabled veterans are living
  on Department of Veterans Affairs disability compensation, and they have other bills.
  Remind them that they can take time to pay it, because DAV understands that a lot
  of disabled veterans don’t have top-paying jobs, and they need more time to pay
  off a membership.”</p>\n<p>Guidance for recruiting new DAV members is available
  at dav.org under Member Resources. Click on the Publicity tile to find our recruitment
  resources, available in digital, print and video formats.</p>\n\n</div><p>The post

Sign up one fully paid new member of the Disabled American Veterans (DAV) and enter to win a prize package for the 2026 DAV and Auxiliary National Convention in Orlando, Florida. The package includes $2,500 in spending money and hotel accommodations. To participate, recruit a new member by June 30, 2026. Each person can make one entry regardless of how many members they recruit. A drawing will take place after the deadline to select the winner.

National Adjutant Barry Jesinoski emphasizes the importance of reaching a recruitment goal of one million members for legislative strength. He encourages members to focus on recruiting one veteran to empower them personally. Additionally, those who recruit multiple members will receive credits for the DAV Store and other awards.

Shannon Sander, a successful recruiter, suggests using empathy and personal passion to connect with potential members. She advises applicants to view rejection as an opportunity for further conversation and to be flexible with payment options, understanding the financial challenges many disabled veterans face. Resources for recruitment are available on the DAV website.
