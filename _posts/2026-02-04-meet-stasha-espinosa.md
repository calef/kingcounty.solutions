  We asked Stasha ten questions to learn more about her background, community, and
  how she connects to our work at Inatai.</p>\n<p><strong>Name: </strong>Stasha Espinosa<br><strong>Pronouns:</strong>
  she/her/hers<br><strong>Role: </strong> Policy &amp; Advocacy Deputy Director<br><strong>Home
  County: </strong> South Tacoma, Pierce County</p>\n<p><strong>1. What are three
  words that describe you?</strong></p>\n<p>Focused, kind, direct.</p>\n<p><strong>2.
  What is the “long story short” of how you landed at Inatai?</strong></p>\n<p>I’ve
  spent nearly 20 years in Washington helping elected leaders at every level of government
  make community-informed decisions. Often, I’ve been the squeaky bridge bringing
  rural and BIPOC communities into rooms where they were not initially centered. This
  work is important to me because I grew up in extreme poverty and I know policy is
  not abstract. It decides whether families can stay housed, eat, heal, and plan for
  the future. I started paying close attention to Inatai after hearing President &amp;
  CEO Nichole June Maher speak at a conference. Learning about Inatai’s 50-Year Vision
  stuck with me. When this role opened, I applied.</p>\n<p><strong>3. What excites
  you most about being part of Team Inatai?</strong></p>\n<p>Inatai plans in 50-year
  increments in a world of 24-hour news cycles. That’s my kind of math. We’re watching
  the rapid growth of seeds of inequity and the destruction of democracy. This was
  always the plan and it’s starting to show up louder in Washington. I’m laser focused
  on contributing to Inatai’s work as a political organization that unapologetically
  uses its resources and influence to build lasting community power and dismantle
  racist systems.</p>\n<p><strong>4. Tell us about the community or communities you
  belong to.</strong></p>\n<p>I am a proud mama raising a family in South Tacoma.
  I was raised in both Catholic and Muslim faiths, which taught me how to listen across
  difference and that we have more in common than we think. My maternal grandparents
  are Aeta, a marginalized Indigenous people of the Philippines, and my paternal grandparents
  include African American civil rights activists. I am grateful for their strength
  and wisdom, and for the reflections of them in my children.</p>\n<p><strong>5. Inatai’s
  strategies are underlined by a 50-Year Vision informed by communities. What is it
  about your community that keeps you hopeful for the future?</strong></p>\n<p>I’m
  hopeful because I see parents in Tacoma raising children like the future is a shared
  responsibility. Despite everything that has happened to separate people from each
  other, I see examples at the parks, libraries, and other community spaces that show
  life and joy—especially at our brand-new Asia Pacific Cultural Center.</p>\n<p><strong>6.
  Finish this sentence: The Inatai value the most resonates with me is…</strong></p>\n<p>Joy.</p>\n<p><strong>7.
  What is your favorite fact about Washington?  </strong></p>\n<p>Washington is one
  of just about half of states allow citizens to put initiatives or referendums on
  the ballot. Key components of who we are as a state have been decided by these citizen-validated
  efforts, including: marriage equality, paid sick leave, abortion protections, and
  recreational cannabis.</p>\n<p><strong>8. Where is your favorite place in Washington?</strong></p>\n<p>I
  love to catch the sunset at Scenic Beach in Seabeck. Bearing witness to how the
  mountains cradle the earth and water is an easy reminder that there is life in all
  things and a beginning after each ending.</p>\n<p><strong>9. What is the last thing
  you read, watched, or listened to that you would recommend? Why?</strong></p>\n<p>Always:
  Dr. Tema Okun’s book, “White Supremacy Culture: Characteristics and Antidotes.”
  Read it, reread it, forward it, and send it out with your holiday card.</p>\n<p><strong>10.
  What do you like to do when you’re not at work?</strong></p>\n<p>Love up my family,
  read, and garden.</p>\n</div>\n\t\t\t</div>\n\t\t\t</div>\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t</div>\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t\t\n\t\t\t</div>"

Stasha Espinosa has joined the Inatai Foundation as the Policy & Advocacy Deputy Director. With nearly 20 years of experience in Washington, she has worked to ensure that rural and BIPOC communities have a voice in government decisions. Espinosa's commitment to community-informed policy stems from her own upbringing in extreme poverty, where she learned firsthand the impact of policy on families' lives. She was inspired to apply for her new role after hearing Inatai's President & CEO speak at a conference and resonating with the foundation's 50-Year Vision.

Espinosa is a proud mother from South Tacoma and has a rich cultural background, with Aeta Indigenous and African American civil rights heritage. She finds hope in her community, where she sees collective efforts among parents to raise children with a sense of shared responsibility. Espinosa values joy as a core principle and appreciates Washington’s unique citizen-led initiatives, including marriage equality and paid sick leave. In her free time, she enjoys gardening, reading, and spending time with her family.
