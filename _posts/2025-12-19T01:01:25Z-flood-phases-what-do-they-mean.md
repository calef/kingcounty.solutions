  <p>This blog post was originally scheduled to be our 2025 Year-End Review; a look back at the progress, partnerships, and investments made over the past year to reduce flood risk across King County. However, with widespread and ongoing flooding currently impacting communities throughout King County, we’ve made the decision to pause that reflection.</p>



  <p>The 2025 Year-End Review will now be published on December 30.</p>



  <p>Today’s post instead focuses on what is happening right now:</p>



  <ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li>How King County defines flood phases, and</li>



  <li>What flooding looks like across our rivers, by comparing normal conditions to the current flood event.</li>
  </ul>



  <p>Our priority during active flood events is clear communication, public safety, and supporting coordination efforts across jurisdictions as communities respond and recover.</p>



  <h2 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-center"><strong>Understanding Flood Phases in King County</strong></h2>



  <p>King County uses flood phases to reflect river flood conditions. The higher the phase, the more water flows in our rivers, and the greater the likelihood of flooding. These phases are based on historical flood conditions unique to each river basin, helping emergency managers, public agencies, and residents understand the severity of an event.</p>



  <h3 class="wp-block-heading">Flood Phases Explained</h3>



  <ul class="wp-block-list">
  <li>Flood Phase 0 – No flooding expected<br>Rivers are within normal ranges.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 1 – Be on alert<br>Conditions are being closely monitored, and preparations begin for a possible flood event.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 2 – Minor flooding expected<br>Low-lying areas may flood, and impacts begin to occur.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 3 – Moderate flooding expected<br>Floodwaters may begin to affect homes, roads, and infrastructure.</li>



  <li>Flood Phase 4 – Severe flooding expected<br>Significant flooding with potentially threatening impacts to life, property, and critical infrastructure.</li>
  </ul>



  <p>During the current event, multiple river basins across King County have reached high flood phases, underscoring the seriousness of this situation.</p>



  <h2 class="wp-block-heading has-text-align-center"><strong>A Comparison of Rivers in Different Flood Phases</strong></h2>



  <p>The images below show rivers across King County under non-flood conditions, when water remains within its banks and ecosystems function as expected. These are contrasted with images showing the same rivers during the ongoing flood event, when prolonged rainfall has pushed flows well beyond normal levels, impacting nearby communities, farmland, roads, and infrastructure.</p>



  <p>These side-by-side comparisons highlight how dramatically river conditions can change during major storm systems and why flood preparedness, early warning, and long-term flood risk reduction remain essential.</p>

King County is currently experiencing widespread flooding, prompting a shift in focus from its planned 2025 Year-End Review to urgent flood communications. The county utilizes a flood phase system to indicate river conditions, ranging from Phase 0 (no flooding expected) to Phase 4 (severe flooding anticipated). Each phase reflects historical data specific to river basins, aiding emergency management and public awareness.

During this ongoing flood event, several river basins have reached higher flood phases, indicating significant risk to nearby communities. The county emphasizes the importance of clear communication and public safety while supporting coordinated recovery efforts. Visual comparisons illustrate the dramatic changes in river conditions due to prolonged rainfall, underscoring the need for preparedness and effective flood risk reduction strategies.

Post-flood, a detailed report will be released, outlining data on river levels, flood impacts, and response actions taken throughout the event. In the meantime, King County officials urge residents to remain vigilant, prioritize safety, and support one another during this crisis as they work to manage the situation effectively.
