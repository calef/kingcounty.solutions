A randomized attention-controlled trial is underway to assess the effectiveness of the MY-RIDE intervention aimed at improving HIV prevention and reducing substance use among homeless youth aged 18-25. This study targets the higher risk of HIV infection and the prevalence of suicide and overdose in this population. MY-RIDE was co-designed with input from homeless youth and is based on the Information-Motivation-Behavioral Skills Model. It includes an individual session led by a nurse focused on HIV prevention, followed by three months of personalized messaging delivered via phone based on the youth's current risk level. Participants also have access to a nurse helpline through an app. The study aims to enroll 320 participants, with recruitment beginning in fall 2024 at various organizations serving homeless youth. Participants will complete surveys and HIV/sexually transmitted infection testing at multiple intervals over a year to evaluate the intervention's impact on HIV prevention strategies and substance use. As of now, 192 participants have been enrolled. The study will also explore the intervention's influence on factors like willingness to use pre-exposure prophylaxis and access to mental health services.
