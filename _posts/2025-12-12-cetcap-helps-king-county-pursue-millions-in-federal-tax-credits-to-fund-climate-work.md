  <h2>Project overview</h2>



  <p>King County is working to meet its climate goals and, with the support from Commerce’s CETCAP program, recently filed for $6.5 million in federal energy tax credit for several clean energy projects. These filings include:</p>



  <ul>
  <li>
  <strong> $4.6 million for a battery storage system</strong> at the West Point Treatment Plant, which will also prevent potential pollution into Puget Sound.</li>



  <li>
  <strong>$1.5 million for adding</strong> <strong>200 light-duty electric vehicles</strong> to its fleet.</li>



  <li>
  <strong>$285,000 for three solar projects </strong>at wastewater treatment and solid waste facilities.</li>



  <li>
  <strong>$88,000 for nine charging ports </strong>at a King County Metro bus base.</li>
  </ul>



  <h2>Technical assistance provided </h2>



  <p>King County received extensive support from Giraffe Financial to file documents properly and ensure the County could secure the maximum possible credit. Lawyers for Good Government provided ongoing legal support to navigate credit eligibility and understand complicated federal rules. This support both saved the County significant money to file the 600+ page return and the burden of identifying and procuring tax and legal expertise.</p>



  <h2>Outcomes and impact </h2>



  <p>King County has set ambitious goals to set greenhouse gas emissions, prepare for climate impacts, and support sustainable, resilient frontline communities. The County actively seeks state and federal funding to help meet these goals. Claiming federal tax credits allows the County to recover some of its project costs, freeing up funds to continue and expand this work in the future.</p>

King County is seeking $6.5 million in federal energy tax credits to fund clean energy projects, supported by the Washington Clean Energy Tax Credit Assistance Program (CETCAP). The proposed funding includes $4.6 million for a battery storage system at the West Point Treatment Plant, which aims to reduce pollution in Puget Sound, $1.5 million for 200 electric vehicles, $285,000 for three solar projects at wastewater treatment and solid waste facilities, and $88,000 for nine charging ports at a King County Metro bus base.

Giraffe Financial provided technical assistance to ensure accurate filing of the complex paperwork, while Lawyers for Good Government offered legal support to navigate eligibility and federal regulations. This collaboration helped save King County significant costs related to the filing process.

These efforts are part of King County's broader goal to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and build climate resilience. Successfully claiming these federal tax credits will enable the County to recover some project costs, allowing for the continuation and expansion of sustainability initiatives. CETCAP has assisted over 150 organizations statewide in securing tax credits, enhancing funding opportunities for clean energy projects across Washington.
