  \           <div>\n<p><strong>For immediate release</strong>: January 5, 2026  
  (26-001)</p>\n\n<p><strong>Contact</strong>: <a>DOH Communications</a><br>\nPublic
  inquiries: Health Systems Customer Service 360-236-4700</p>\n\n<div>\n<p><strong>OLYMPIA</strong>
  -- The Washington State Department of Health has taken disciplinary actions or withdrawn
  charges against the following health care providers in our state.</p>\n\n<p>The
  department’s Health Systems Quality Assurance Division works with boards, commissions,
  and advisory committees to set licensing standards for more than 80 health care
  professions (e.g., dentists, nursing assistants, counselors).</p>\n\n<p>Information
  about health care providers is on the agency website. Click on “<a>Health Care Provider
  Lookup</a>” under the “Find it Fast” section of the <a>Department of Health website</a>
  (doh.wa.gov).The site includes information about a health care provider’s license
  status, the expiration and renewal date of their credential, disciplinary actions
  and copies of legal documents issued after July 1998. This information is also available
  by calling 360-236-4700. Consumers who think a health care provider acted unprofessionally
  are encouraged to call and report their complaint.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Adams County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health released registered nursing assistant <strong>Yvette
  Verduzco-Alvarez </strong>(NA60628958) from the terms and conditions of a 2024 agreed
  order and terminated monitoring.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Chelan County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health charged agency affiliated counselor <strong>Steven
  Mario Perez</strong> (CG61276108) with unprofessional conduct. In June 2025, Perez
  was convicted of vehicular assault–intoxicating liquor or any drug and attempting
  to elude, class B and C felonies. Perez did not respond to the department’s request
  for information.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Clark County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In October
  2025 the Department of Health denied <strong>Brandon Aune’s</strong> (NA61551315)
  application for a registered nursing assistant credential. Aune is prohibited from
  practicing due to disciplinary action taken against his nursing assistant credential
  in Oregon in August 2019.</p>\n\n<p>In April 2025 the Dental Quality Assurance Commission
  charged dentist <strong>Brady Tucker Smith </strong>(DE60506244) with unprofessional
  conduct. Smith allegedly failed to comply with the terms and conditions of a 2022
  agreed order, including completing required continuing education and meeting chart
  audit requirements. In November 2025, Tucker entered a new agreed order with the
  Commission and is subject to its terms and conditions, including paying a $25,000
  fine.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Cowlitz County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the
  Department of Health terminated the probation on <strong>Megan Elizabeth McNemar’s</strong>
  (CB61388975) certified behavior technician credential.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>King County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Pharmacy Quality Assurance Commission released pharmacy technician
  <strong>Connie Hy Taylor</strong> (VA00041354) from the terms and conditions of
  her 2020 agreed order and terminated monitoring.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the
  Department of Health terminated the probation on <strong>Rachel Cecelia Engh’s</strong>
  (CG61421344, MC61465662) associate mental health counselor and registered agency
  affiliated counselor credentials.</p>\n\n<p>In September 2025 the Pharmacy Quality
  Assurance Commission charged pharmacy assistant <strong>Akira Trudie Jackson</strong>
  (VB61360910) with unprofessional conduct. In August 2025, Jackson allegedly failed
  to comply with a 2025 agreed order.</p>\n\n<p>In October 2025 the Department of
  Health charged registered nursing assistant <strong>Alimamy Dauda Kamara </strong>(NA60018375)
  with unprofessional conduct. Between August and October 2023, Kamara allegedly used
  funds from a vulnerable adult resident’s bank account to purchase items for the
  adult family home he operated.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health
  charged<strong> Fei Li</strong> with the unlicensed practice of massage therapy
  and notified her of its intent to issue a cease-and-desist order. Li does not hold,
  and has never held, a credential to practice massage therapy in Washington state.
  In April 2025, Li confirmed to a department investigator she provided massage services
  and quoted prices.</p>\n\n<p>In August 2025 the Department of Health notified <strong>Tanieka
  L. Minor</strong> (NC60148441) that her certified nursing assistant credential was
  issued in error. Minor did not provide required documentation showing completion
  of an approved training program or equivalent credentials. Her credential was set
  to be rescinded and moved to pending status unless she requested a hearing by Sept.
  9, 2025, to show she had satisfied the necessary requirements for licensure.</p>\n\n<p>In
  September 2025 the Dental Quality Assurance Commission and dentist<strong> Lance
  B. Timmerman</strong> (DE00008229) entered an agreed order that supersedes his November
  2021 agreed order. Timmerman must comply with all terms and conditions of the order,
  including paying a $20,000 fine and completing a monitoring program.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Pierce
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health and <strong>Jason
  Brown</strong> entered an agreed order requiring Brown to permanently cease and
  desist from practicing massage therapy unless first obtaining the proper credential
  or qualifying for an exception. Brown must comply with all terms and conditions
  of the order, including paying a $1,000 fine.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department
  of Health charged substance use disorder professional trainee <strong>Sherilyn Ann
  Finlayson</strong> (CO61468639) with unprofessional conduct. Between July 2023 and
  November 2024, Finlayson allegedly provided services without proper training or
  supervision. In December 2024, she submitted a worklog for July and August 2023,
  but the reported hours were inconsistent with her supervisor’s information. In June
  2024, Finlayson was assigned a new supervisor and allegedly received multiple warnings
  for untimely or incomplete documentation and for missing required meetings.</p>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health charged independent social worker <strong>Tamara
  Kristin Pietzke</strong> (LW60715945) with unprofessional conduct. In January 2024,
  Pietzke allegedly accessed two former patients’ medical records without authorization
  and the following month published an article that included potentially identifying
  information about former patients.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Skagit County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health and <strong>Shari Arvilla Lambertz </strong>(NC60078638)
  entered an agreed order that reinstated Lambertz’s certified nursing assistant credential
  with conditions, including at least three years of probation. In her application,
  Lambertz failed to disclose a February 2006 conviction for third-degree malicious
  mischief, a gross misdemeanor. Between July and August 2022, Lambertz also accepted
  $6,000 from a patient.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Snohomish County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Pharmacy Quality Assurance Commission and <strong>Saikrishna Arumilli
  </strong>(PH00039402) entered an agreed order placing Arumilli’s pharmacist credential
  on probation for at least three years and prohibiting him from serving as a responsible
  pharmacy manager. Arumilli must comply with all terms and conditions of the order,
  including paying a $2,000 fine.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Spokane County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health granted <strong>Marian Alexander Bennett’s</strong>
  (CP70052529) request for a substance use disorder professional credential with conditions,
  including probation through at least Dec. 12, 2026.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the
  Department of Health terminated the probation on <strong>Brandon M. Coppin’s</strong>
  (CG61407017) registered agency affiliated counselor credential.</p>\n\n<p>In October
  2025 the Department of Health granted <strong>Katelynn Elizabeth Dennis’</strong>
  (CP61373972) application for a substance use disorder professional credential, subject
  to meeting regulatory requirements and the conditions of her 2024 stipulation.</p>\n</div>\n\n<h2><strong>Whatcom
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Department of Health charged certified
  medical assistant <strong>Nichole Dawn Hernandez</strong> (CM60715085) with unprofessional
  conduct. In September 2023, Hernandez allegedly accessed a minor patient’s medical
  records without authorization and subsequently disclosed the minor’s protected health
  information to an individual outside the medical facility without authorization.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Yakima
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Dental Quality Assurance Commission
  released dentist <strong>Adam Zigmund Mileski </strong>(DE60601618) from the terms
  and conditions of his 2025 agreed order and terminated monitoring.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Out
  of State</strong></h2>\n\n<p><strong>Idaho</strong>: In October 2025 the Department
  of Health denied <strong>Italia Marie Roberts-Almond’s </strong>(HM61544756) application
  for a home care aide credential. In July 2025, Roberts-Almond was placed on the
  Department of Social and Health Services’ long-term care abuse and neglect registry,
  which disqualifies her from working with vulnerable adults and from being certified
  or maintaining certification as a home care aide.</p>\n\n<p><strong>Nevada</strong>:
  In December 2025 the Department of Health charged certified counselor and hypnotist
  <strong>Patricia Diane Sewell</strong> (CL60162229, HP10001373) with unprofessional
  conduct. Between May and October 2021, Sewell allegedly failed to properly document
  a client’s request not to keep treatment records and signed a disclosure form on
  the client’s behalf without authorization. Despite the request, Sewell maintained
  treatment records.<br>\n<br>\n<strong>Oregon</strong>: In October 2025 the Department
  of Health denied<strong> Berta Dolores Delgado’s</strong> (HM61479475) application
  for a home care aide credential. Delgado was placed on the Department of Social
  and Health Services’ long-term care abuse and neglect registry in November 2024,
  which disqualifies her from working with vulnerable adults and from being certified
  or maintaining certification as a home care aide.</p>\n\n<p><strong>Note to Editors</strong>:
  Health care providers charged with unprofessional conduct have 20 days to respond
  to the Department of Health in writing. The case then enters the settlement process.
  If no disciplinary agreement can be reached, the case will go to a hearing.</p>\n\n<p>Our
  <a>website</a> is your source for a healthy dose of information. Get updates by
  following us on <a>social media</a>.</p>\n\n<p>###</p>\n</div>\n      \n  <div>\n

The Washington State Department of Health has announced disciplinary actions against various health care providers as of January 5, 2026. Actions include the release of registered nursing assistant Yvette Verduzco-Alvarez from monitoring and the charging of counselor Steven Mario Perez with unprofessional conduct following a vehicular assault conviction. The Department denied Brandon Aune’s application for a nursing assistant credential due to prior disciplinary action in Oregon. Dentist Brady Tucker Smith was charged with unprofessional conduct for failing to meet agreed order terms and was fined $25,000. Other disciplinary actions include charges against pharmacy assistant Akira Trudie Jackson for noncompliance, and registered nursing assistant Alimamy Dauda Kamara for misusing funds from a vulnerable adult. The Department also charged Fei Li for practicing massage therapy without a license. In total, several providers from various counties faced charges or had their credentials affected due to unprofessional conduct or failure to meet licensing requirements. Consumers can check the status of health care providers and report complaints through the Department of Health website or by phone.
