A study evaluated the effectiveness of three topical treatments for cats with dermatophytosis caused by _Microsporum canis_ in animal shelters. The trial compared miconazole chlorhexidine (MC), hydrogen peroxide (HP), and lime sulfur (LS) shampoos. A total of 76 cats were randomized into treatment groups while receiving oral itraconazole. Treatment success was defined as mycological cure within seven weeks. Results showed that LS significantly outperformed both MC and HP, with a mean cure time of 27 days compared to 37 days for MC and 36 days for HP. Although the alternative treatments took longer, their inferiority was not conclusively proven. A Cox proportional hazards analysis indicated that both MC and HP performed significantly worse than LS. Additionally, younger cats required more time to achieve a cure. Shelter staff reported no differences in the unpleasantness, difficulty, or reactions of cats to the treatments. The study suggests that LS is the most effective option for treating _M. canis_ in shelter cats, while HP may be a viable alternative if LS is unavailable.
