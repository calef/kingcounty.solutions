In response to the historic flooding that occurred in December 2025, the King County Flood Control District (KCFCD) Board of Supervisors approved a $10 million funding package to address the emergency response and conduct a comprehensive study on the downstream impacts of water releases from the Howard Hanson Dam. The floods, triggered by a series of atmospheric rivers, led to significant damage to homes, businesses, and agricultural lands, though no lives were lost due to proactive flood control measures. Board Supervisor Steffanie Fain emphasized the need for transparency and accountability, as the study aims to analyze the effects of dam operations during the floods, including rapid drawdowns and water release strategies. This initiative is intended to enhance preparedness for future flooding events. The KCFCD will report on the study’s progress at a meeting scheduled for March 4, 2026. Following the briefing from the King County Department of Natural Resources and Parks and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, the Board also directed the KCFCD to develop options for 2026 grant programs focused on flood response. The December flooding was unprecedented, leading to widespread evacuations and a state of emergency across King County and surrounding regions.
