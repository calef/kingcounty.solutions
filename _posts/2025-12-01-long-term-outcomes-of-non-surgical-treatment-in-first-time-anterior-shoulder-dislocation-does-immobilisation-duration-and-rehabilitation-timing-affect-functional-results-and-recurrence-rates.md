A study examined how immobilization duration and rehabilitation timing affect recovery in patients with first-time anterior shoulder dislocation. Conducted across multiple hospitals from 2015 to 2021, the research involved 201 non-surgically treated patients divided into three groups based on their immobilization and rehabilitation schedules. Group 1 underwent early mobilization with one to two weeks of immobilization, Group 2 followed a standard protocol with three to four weeks, and Group 3 had delayed rehabilitation after more than four weeks of immobilization.

Results showed no significant differences in functional scores or range of motion among the groups. Recurrence rates were 18% for Group 1, 23.7% for Group 2, and 23.4% for Group 3, with no statistical significance. However, treatment failure, defined as ongoing instability or the need for surgery, was notably higher in Group 3 at 23.4%, compared to 16.4% for Group 1 and 18.4% for Group 2. The study concluded that prolonged immobilization increases the risk of treatment failure, suggesting that early or standard rehabilitation protocols may be more effective. Further research is recommended to establish optimal treatment strategies.
