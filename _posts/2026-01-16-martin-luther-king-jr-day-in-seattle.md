title: Martin Luther King, Jr. Day in Seattle

---

Seattle has recognized Martin Luther King, Jr. Day since December 28, 1970, when the City Council passed Resolution 22872, declaring January 15 a city holiday in honor of King’s contributions to humanity. The resolution was sent to Dr. King's widow, Coretta Scott King, Washington Governor Dan Evans, and the State Legislature, who all responded positively. On January 8, 1971, Mayor Wes Uhlman and Governor Evans issued proclamations to celebrate King’s birthday. In 1973, the City Council passed Resolution 23982, urging the state to recognize the day as a legal holiday. A letter from Councilmember Sam Smith acknowledged the inspiration King provided, emphasizing the importance of their actions. The holiday became federal law in November 1983, leading Seattle to officially observe it on the third Monday in January starting in 1984. The legislation was supported by public testimony from local leaders. In 1994, Seattle initiated the annual “Pieces of the Dream” Diversity Award Ceremony, and the city has since hosted various events like marches and workshops to celebrate King's legacy. A virtual collection highlights Seattle's MLK Day celebrations and honors his memory.
