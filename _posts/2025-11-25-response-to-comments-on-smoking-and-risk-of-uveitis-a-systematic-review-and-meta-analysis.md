A recent meta-analysis reaffirms that smoking is a significant modifiable risk factor for uveitis, with evidence showing increased disease rates across various subtypes. Mechanisms such as oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, and cytokine activation provide biological plausibility for this association. Additionally, smoking negatively impacts treatment responses in patients with immune-mediated diseases, including psoriasis, Crohn's disease, and rheumatoid arthritis, leading to lower chances of remission after biologic therapy. Tobacco use is linked to systemic organ damage, affecting cardiovascular, pulmonary, and ocular health, including conditions like macular edema. The study notes variability in exposure assessments and the lack of comprehensive data on e-cigarettes, indicating a need for further investigation. Clinicians are urged to counsel patients on quitting smoking or vaping to enhance treatment effectiveness, reduce inflammation, and prevent disease recurrence. Ophthalmologists are highlighted as crucial in preventing uveitis and improving patient outcomes.
