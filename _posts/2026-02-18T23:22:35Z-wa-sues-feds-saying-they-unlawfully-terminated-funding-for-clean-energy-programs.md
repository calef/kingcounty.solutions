Washington state is co-leading a lawsuit against the federal government, claiming it unlawfully terminated funding for clean energy initiatives mandated by Congress. Announced by Attorney General Nick Brown, the lawsuit involves 14 states and targets actions by the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) and Energy Secretary Chris Wright. The states assert that funds from the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act and the Inflation Reduction Act, both passed under the Biden administration, have gone unspent due to the DOE's inaction. A significant part of the lawsuit focuses on $2.2 billion allocated to the Regional Hydrogen Hub program, including the Pacific Northwest Hydrogen Hub, which the DOE cut funding for on October 1, 2025. The lawsuit contends that the DOE also terminated four grants to Washington State University without justification, which were aimed at developing low-carbon concrete and grid support projects. The states allege that the DOE's actions were influenced by a Trump-era executive order and that the agency lacks the constitutional authority to defund programs established by Congress. The lawsuit seeks to hold the federal government accountable for what the states describe as an attempt to undermine progress in renewable energy and climate change initiatives.
