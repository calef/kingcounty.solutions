Spending time in nature has been shown to provide various health benefits, including reduced cortisol levels, enhanced immune function, decreased depression, improved attention, lower blood pressure, and better sleep quality. Research indicates that even short outings in green spaces can be healing, prompting some healthcare providers to prescribe nature time as part of treatment. While the exact mechanisms behind these benefits are unclear, factors such as physical activity, exposure to natural light, and improved air quality are thought to contribute.

One effective method to harness nature's healing power is "forest bathing," a Japanese practice involving silent, guided walks through forests, focusing on sensory experiences. Individuals can also practice mindful observation in local parks or natural settings, even for as little as two hours a week, which can yield significant restorative effects.

Moreover, combining nature outings with social connections may enhance benefits, particularly for older adults. The article encourages individuals to engage with nature according to their physical abilities, suggesting that even short, regular visits can improve overall well-being and mood.
