  </strong>-- The Washington State Department of Health has taken disciplinary actions
  or withdrawn charges against the following health care providers in our state.</p>\n\n<p>The
  department’s Health Systems Quality Assurance Division works with boards, commissions,
  and advisory committees to set licensing standards for more than 80 health care
  professions (e.g., dentists, nurses, counselors). Information about disciplinary
  action taken against medical doctors and physician assistants can be found on the
  <a>Washington Medical Commission (WMC) website</a>. Questions about WMC disciplinary
  actions can be sent to <a>media@wmc.wa.gov</a>.</p>\n\n<p>Information about health
  care providers is on the agency website. Click on “<a>Health Care Provider Lookup</a>”
  under the “Find it Fast” section of the <a>Department of Health website</a> (doh.wa.gov).The
  site includes information about a health care provider’s license status, the expiration
  and renewal date of their credential, disciplinary actions and copies of legal documents
  issued after July 1998. This information is also available by calling 360-236-4700.
  Consumers who think a health care provider acted unprofessionally are encouraged
  to call and report their complaint.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Cowlitz County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In
  December 2025 the Department of Health charged certified medical assistant<strong>
  Chad Edmond Broderius</strong> (CM60438009) with unprofessional conduct. In April
  2024, the department received a complaint alleging Broderius worked outside his
  scope of practice by mixing two medications at a doctor’s request. He did not respond
  to the department’s request for information.</p>\n\n<p>In November 2025 the Department
  of Health terminated the probation on <strong>Kendra Nicole Disspain’s </strong>(CO61589685)
  substance use disorder professional trainee credential.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>King
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In August 2025 the Department of Health notified <strong>Joan
  Elizabeth Aquino</strong> (NC60430942) that her certified nursing assistant credential
  was issued in error. Aquino did not provide required documentation showing completion
  of an approved training program or equivalent credentials. Her credential was set
  to be rescinded and moved to pending status unless she requested a hearing by Sept.
  9, 2025, to show she had satisfied the necessary requirements for licensure.</p>\n\n<p>In
  November 2025 the Pharmacy Quality Assurance Commission charged pharmacy assistant
  and pharmacy technician <strong>Ashley Browning</strong> (VB61249559, VA61352438)
  with unprofessional conduct. In January 2024, Browning entered an agreement requiring
  her to comply with a monitoring program and appear before the Commission within
  12 months.</p>\n\n<p>In October 2025 the Dental Quality Assurance Commission denied
  <strong>Elizabeth Ann Currier’s</strong> (D170004874) application for a dental assistant
  credential. Currier was convicted of fourth-degree assault, a gross misdemeanor,
  in March 2025, and of first-degree criminal trespass 1, also a gross misdemeanor,
  in August 2024.</p>\n\n<p>In November 2025 the Dental Quality Assurance Commission
  charged <strong>Wei Guo</strong> (GA60168914, DE60137855), a dentist with a general
  anesthesia permit, with unprofessional conduct. In July 2021, Guo allegedly failed
  to follow a patient’s sedation precautions, administered excessive anesthesia, and
  failed to monitor vital signs, resulting in the patient being hospitalized. Guo
  also allegedly failed to report the adverse event. Separately, between February
  2022 and March 2023, Guo allegedly submitted an improperly created referral slip
  for another patient and misrepresented that it had been sent to the medical provider.</p>\n\n<p>In
  October 2025 the Department of Health denied<strong> Thomas Mahoney’s </strong>(CO61659778)
  application for a substance use disorder professional credential. Mahoney was charged
  in January 2019 with three counts of second-degree felony travel with intent to
  engage in a sexual act with a minor and one count of third-degree felony enticement
  of a minor.</p>\n\n<p>In December 2025 the Dental Quality Assurance Commission released
  dental technician <strong>Connie Hy Taylor</strong> (VA00041354) from the terms
  and conditions of her 2020 agreed order and terminated monitoring.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Kittitas
  County</strong></h2>\n\n<p>In November 2025 the Department of Health denied<strong>
  Jordon Bradley Spiese’s</strong> (HM61498889) application for a home care aide credential.
  Spiese was placed on the Department of Social and Health Services’ long-term care
  abuse and neglect registry in April 2025, which disqualifies him from working with
  vulnerable adults and being certified as a home care aide.</p>\n\n<h2><strong>Mason

The Washington State Department of Health has announced various disciplinary actions against health care providers across the state. In December 2025, certified medical assistant Chad Edmond Broderius was charged with unprofessional conduct for working beyond his scope of practice. Kendra Nicole Disspain had her probation terminated, while Joan Elizabeth Aquino’s nursing assistant credential was issued in error due to missing training documentation. Ashley Browning faced charges for unprofessional conduct after failing to comply with a monitoring program. The Dental Quality Assurance Commission denied Elizabeth Ann Currier's application for a dental assistant credential due to criminal convictions and charged dentist Wei Guo with unprofessional conduct for failing to monitor a patient properly. Other cases included denials of home care aide credentials for Jordon Bradley Spiese and Pamela Jean Stiles, who were listed on the abuse and neglect registry. The department also placed massage therapist Jennifer Diana Ortiz on probation and granted agency affiliated counselor credentials to several individuals under specific conditions. Health care providers have 20 days to respond to charges, with unresolved cases proceeding to a hearing.
