A study conducted at Mardan Medical Complex evaluated the long-term outcomes of non-surgical management for midshaft clavicle fractures. Over 24 months, 110 patients with closed middle-third clavicle fractures were treated conservatively. Functional outcomes were assessed using the QuickDASH and Constant-Murley Shoulder Score (CMSS) after 12 months. Out of 110 patients, 102 completed follow-up. Union occurred in 88 patients (86.3%) within 16 weeks, while nonunion and malunion were noted in 7 (6.9%) and 10 (9.8%) patients, respectively. The average QuickDASH score was 9.6, indicating minimal disability, and the average CMSS was 88.2, reflecting good shoulder function. Patients who achieved union reported significantly better functional scores. Cosmetic dissatisfaction was reported by 18 patients (17.6%), primarily linked to malunion. The findings suggest that conservative treatment for midshaft clavicle fractures is largely effective, but the quality of healing significantly impacts functional outcomes. Proper patient selection and regular follow-up are crucial to reduce complications and enhance recovery.
