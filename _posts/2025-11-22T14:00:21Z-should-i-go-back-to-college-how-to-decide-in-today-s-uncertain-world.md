As the job market evolves due to advancements in artificial intelligence and the rise of remote work, many adults are contemplating whether to return to college. The decision to pursue higher education can lead to career advancement, higher earning potential—bachelor’s degree holders earn an average of 86% more than high school graduates—and personal fulfillment. However, potential students must weigh the pros and cons, including the high costs of tuition and the time commitment required for classes and studying.

Financial considerations are significant, with average in-state public university tuition around $9,750 per year, and many individuals are wary of student debt. There are various financial aid options available, including federal aid, scholarships, and employer tuition reimbursement programs.

Those considering a return to school should assess their motivations and life circumstances, such as current commitments and career goals. Flexible learning options, including online and hybrid courses, have become more available, allowing students to balance education with work and personal responsibilities. Institutions like City University of Seattle offer supportive resources tailored for adult learners, emphasizing that college opportunities are accessible for everyone, regardless of age or background.
