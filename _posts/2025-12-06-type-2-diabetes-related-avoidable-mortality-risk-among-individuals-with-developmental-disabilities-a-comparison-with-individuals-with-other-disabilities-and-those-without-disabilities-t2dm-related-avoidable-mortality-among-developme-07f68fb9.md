A study assessed type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM)-related avoidable mortality in individuals with developmental disabilities, using national claims data. The cohort included individuals over 30 diagnosed with T2DM between 2012 and 2016, with matched controls based on sex, age, income, and region. The study found that T2DM-related avoidable mortality rates were 0.05%, 0.23%, and 0.55% at 1, 3, and 5 years post-diagnosis for the developmental disability group, which were lower than those for individuals with severe disabilities but higher than for non-disabled individuals. At 3 years, the risk of mortality for those with developmental disabilities was significantly higher compared to non-disabled individuals (odds ratio of 4.84), and at 5 years, the risk remained elevated (odds ratio of 3.82). When compared to individuals with mild disabilities, the 5-year mortality risk was also higher (odds ratio of 2.41). The findings indicate that individuals with developmental disabilities face significant healthcare challenges, necessitating improved access to targeted interventions and support services to reduce avoidable deaths and enhance health outcomes.
