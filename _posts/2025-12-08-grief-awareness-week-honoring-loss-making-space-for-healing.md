  <h2>
  <b><span>December 2 through 8 marks Grief Awareness Week</span></b><span>, a time to acknowledge the many faces of grief and the often-invisible impact it has on mental health. </span>
  </h2>
  <p><span>A</span><span>t Vibrant, we hold space for every person navigating loss, whether recent or long ago, and affirm that grief is not a weakness, but a reflection of love, connection, and humanity.</span><span> </span></p>
  <p><b><span>Grief does not follow a schedule.</span></b><span> It doesn’t expire, and it doesn’t always look like sadness. It may show up as numbness, irritability, fatigue, or even anxiety. Some people feel it in their bodies. Others feel it in everyday silence. Grief can be complicated, delayed, cyclical, and still completely valid.</span><span> </span></p>
  <p><span>People grieve for many reasons: the death of a loved one, the loss of a relationship, a job, a home, or even the loss of a version of life that once was. Grief can come with any event that may shake up our sense of normalcy.  During the holidays, when joy and togetherness are emphasized, grief can feel especially heavy, and the effects of our losses can become even more visible.</span><span> </span></p>
  <h3>
  <span>Grief and Mental Health</span><span> </span>
  </h3>
  <p><span>Grief, while natural, can cause unexpected mental health effects, such as depression, anxiety, physical pain, traumatic stress, or prolonged grief disorder.  These symptoms can be raw at the start or unexpectedly appear. These symptoms can be felt more intensively if grief goes unacknowledged, unsupported, or stigmatized. Many people may try quickly to “move on,” even when they haven’t fully understood the extent of what they’ve lost.</span><span> </span></p>
  <p><span>We must recognize that:</span><span> </span></p>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Grief affects how we function, how we connect, and how we heal.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>There is no right way to grieve, and no roadmap for what will happen after a loss.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Healing does not mean forgetting. It means learning to live with loss and finding personal tools that can assist in this new world.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Community, compassion, and time can be some of the most powerful supports.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <h3>
  <span>You Are Not Alone</span><span> </span>
  </h3>
  <p><span>Grief Awareness Week is a reminder that no one should carry loss in silence. Whether you’re grieving someone who passed years ago or are facing a new loss today, your grief is real, and you don’t have to go through it alone.</span><span> </span></p>
  <p><strong>Let this week be an invitation to: </strong></p>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Check in with yourself and others, especially those who may seem “fine.”</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Tell stories, share memories, or light a candle for those you’ve lost.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Give yourself grace, whatever you’re feeling, or not feeling, is okay.</span><span> </span>
  </li>
  </ul>
  <ul>
  <li>
  <span>Seek out supports that fit your personal needs.</span><span> </span>

December 2 through 8 marks Grief Awareness Week, a time to recognize the various expressions of grief and its significant impact on mental health. At Vibrant, the focus is on providing support for those dealing with loss, affirming that grief reflects love and connection rather than weakness.

Grief is unpredictable and can manifest in many ways, including numbness, irritability, or anxiety. It arises from various losses, such as the death of a loved one, the end of a relationship, or significant life changes. During the holidays, feelings of grief may intensify as the contrast between joy and loss becomes more apparent.

While grief is a natural response, it can lead to mental health challenges like depression or anxiety, especially if unacknowledged. Recognizing that there is no right way to grieve is essential, as is understanding that healing involves learning to live with loss rather than forgetting it. Community support and compassion play crucial roles in the healing process.

Grief Awareness Week encourages individuals to share their feelings, remember those lost, and seek the support they need. Resources are available for those navigating grief, emphasizing that they do not have to face their struggles alone.
