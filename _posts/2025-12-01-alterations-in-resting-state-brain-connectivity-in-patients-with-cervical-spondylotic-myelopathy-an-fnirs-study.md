A study investigated changes in resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) in patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) using functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS). Fifteen CSM patients and 15 healthy controls participated, with fNIRS recordings taken during an eyes-open resting state. The research focused on hemodynamic signals from the prefrontal, parietal, and occipital cortices.

Results showed that CSM patients had significantly enhanced connectivity between the secondary somatosensory cortex and the premotor/somatosensory association cortices. Specifically, connectivity was stronger between the frontal eye field and supramarginal gyrus in CSM patients compared to healthy controls. Additionally, hyperconnectivity was noted between the right occipital lobe and bilateral prefrontal cortices, with pronounced connections to the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex.

The study concluded that these changes may reflect neuroplastic responses to visual and sensorimotor deficits, while also indicating global integration impairments that could contribute to clinical disabilities. The findings provide new insights into functional network changes in CSM, offering quantifiable connectivity markers that could help monitor disease progression and guide therapeutic interventions.
