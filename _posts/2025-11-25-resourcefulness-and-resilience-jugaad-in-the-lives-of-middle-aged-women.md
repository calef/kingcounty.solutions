  **ABSTRACT**

  This exploratory study investigates the creative problem-solving strategies-specifically jugaad-employed by middle-aged women as they navigate the complexities of balancing work, home, financial, and health-related responsibilities. The research aims to understand how women adapt and innovate amidst response to resource constraints to meet competing demands across life domains. A cross-sectional survey for the pilot study was conducted with a sample of 20 women aged 40 and above, born and raised in India but currently residing in diverse urban settings around the world. By focusing on this group, the study aimed to generate preliminary insights into the adaptive behaviors and coping mechanisms shaped by cultural context and lived experiences. Participants completed a self-administered questionnaire capturing both quantitative (Likert-scale and multiple-choice) and qualitative (open-ended) responses on their use of "jugaad"-creative problem-solving strategies-in managing work, home, finances, and health. Data were anonymized, and quantitative responses were analyzed using regression and correlation methods. While qualitative responses were thematically examined to enrich understanding of participants' lived experiences. Findings revealed that age, health concerns, and financial pressures were significant predictors of the use of resourceful strategies - jugaad. Those aged between 40 and 50 were more likely to utilize time management techniques and low-cost solutions. Age, health, and financial pressures significantly impacts the adoption of these strategies. Women relied on health-focused problem-solving strategies. Resourcefulness in the workplace extends to financial decision-making, structured home management enhanced health, and adaptive childcare strategies demand increased effort in meeting unexpected responsibilities.

An exploratory study focused on the problem-solving strategies known as "jugaad" among middle-aged women balancing various responsibilities. The research involved a survey of 20 women, aged 40 and older, who were born in India and now live in different urban areas globally. The goal was to understand how these women adapt to resource constraints in areas such as work, home, finances, and health. Participants completed a questionnaire that included both quantitative and qualitative questions about their use of creative strategies. The analysis revealed that age, health issues, and financial pressures significantly influenced the adoption of these strategies. Women aged 40 to 50 were particularly likely to use time management and low-cost solutions. Findings indicated that health-focused problem-solving is crucial, with resourcefulness in the workplace and financial decision-making linked to effective home management and childcare strategies. Overall, the study highlights the adaptive behaviors shaped by cultural context and personal experiences among these women.
