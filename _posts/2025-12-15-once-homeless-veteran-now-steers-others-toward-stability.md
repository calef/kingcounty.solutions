  <h2>Using bicycle to bridge gap with Veterans</h2>



  <p>When Navy Veteran Christopher Peavy was honorably discharged, he didn’t expect the hardest battle would be the one waiting for him back home.</p>



  <p>“I felt lost. There was no direction in my life and nowhere to turn,” Peavy shared. “I never thought I’d end up homeless.”</p>



  <p>But after serving his country, that’s what happened. With the help of VA, he not only rebuilt his life but also found his purpose.</p>



  <h2>Peavy’s mission and the power of a bike</h2>



  <p>Peavy now works with the <a>Orlando VA Healthcare System (OVAHCS)</a>, dedicated to helping fellow Veterans avoid the same pitfalls he faced. His mission? Connection, and sometimes, that starts with a bike.</p>



  <p>Through a unique program at the Orlando VA, Peavy facilitates the distribution of bicycles to Veterans who need transportation to job interviews, appointments or housing services.</p>



  <p>“A bike might not seem like much,” Peavy said. “But for someone starting from scratch, it represents freedom. It signifies opportunity.”</p>



  <p>The bikes come from donations and a partnership with a local bike shop, which works alongside VA’s <a>homeless program</a> to restore donated bikes. Once repaired and ready, they’re handed off, along with a helmet and lock, to Veterans in need. Peavy personally ensures every recipient knows how to care for the bike, but more importantly, knows someone believes in their journey.</p>



  <p>“I was once on the other side of this,” Peavy shared. “Now, I get to be the one helping. That’s the biggest reward.”</p>



  <p>Peavy often shares his story during outreach events, connecting with Veterans who are hesitant to seek help.</p>



  <p>“They listen because I’ve lived it,” Peavy explained. “I don’t speak from a textbook—I speak from experience.”</p>



  <p>Peavy’s commitment extends beyond just distributing. He follows up with Veterans, checking on their progress and encouraging them to remain engaged with their health care, housing or job placement services.</p>



  <p>“He’s become a bridge,” said Stefanie Mohl, social worker, “turning his past into a powerful source of hope for others.” If you or someone you know needs assistance, please visit the <a>VA Homeless Program</a> for more information.</p>

Navy Veteran Christopher Peavy faced unexpected challenges after his honorable discharge, including homelessness. With help from the VA, he rebuilt his life and now works with the Orlando VA Healthcare System to assist fellow Veterans. Peavy facilitates a program that provides bicycles to Veterans in need, helping them access job interviews, appointments, and housing services. He emphasizes that a bike symbolizes freedom and opportunity for those starting over. The bicycles are restored through donations and a local bike shop partnership, and each comes with a helmet and lock. Peavy ensures recipients know how to maintain their bikes and feel supported in their journeys. He also shares his personal story at outreach events to connect with Veterans who may be reluctant to seek help. His experience makes his message resonate, as he speaks not from a textbook but from lived experience. Peavy remains dedicated to following up with Veterans, encouraging them to engage with health care, housing, and job services. His efforts have made him a bridge of hope for those he helps, according to social worker Stefanie Mohl.
