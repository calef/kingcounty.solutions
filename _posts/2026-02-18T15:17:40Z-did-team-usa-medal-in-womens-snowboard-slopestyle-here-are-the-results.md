At the Milan Cortina Olympics, New Zealand's Zoi Sadowski-Synnott aimed for her second gold medal in women's snowboard slopestyle. The reigning world champion led the qualifying round, while Japan's Kokomo Murase, who had already secured gold in the big air event, qualified second. U.S. snowboarders Jessica Perlmutter and Lily Dhawornvej, both 16 and first-time Olympians, competed in the finals but did not reach the podium. Perlmutter finished 6th after a strong second run, while Dhawornvej placed 11th, struggling with speed in her later attempts. 

Japanese snowboarder Mari Fukada won gold in the slopestyle final, with Sadowski-Synnott earning silver and Murase taking bronze. The event faced criticism from NBC commentators regarding judging inconsistencies. The slopestyle final was initially scheduled for Tuesday but was postponed due to weather concerns, highlighting the sport's vulnerability to changing conditions. Riders perform tricks on a course featuring ramps and rails, a format that has been part of the Olympics since the Sochi Games in 2014.
