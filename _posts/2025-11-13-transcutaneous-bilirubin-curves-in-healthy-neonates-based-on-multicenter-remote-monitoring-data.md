A multicenter retrospective cohort study established transcutaneous bilirubin (TcB) reference curves for healthy neonates up to 30 days old, analyzing regional variations in bilirubin dynamics. The study involved 220,950 healthy neonates born at a gestational age of 35 to 42 weeks, who did not receive phototherapy within 60 hours of birth. Data were collected using the Bilibaby remote jaundice monitoring system from August 2020 to December 2024 across 426 hospitals.

Findings revealed that TcB levels rose rapidly over the first three days, peaking between days four and six at specific percentile values. After day 13, TcB levels stabilized with notable differences observed between southern and northern regions. In the early days, neonates in the south exhibited higher bilirubin levels than those in the north. Among neonates initially classified as low-risk, a significant percentage transitioned to higher-risk categories, with a notable difference in progression rates between regions.

The study provides critical baseline data for managing neonatal jaundice, highlighting the necessity for regional considerations in bilirubin monitoring and intervention strategies.
