  <h2>A legendary celebration     </h2>



  <p>The <a>Tucson </a>VET Center recently celebrated the 102nd birthday of Harvey Horn, a former World War II Prisoner of War and former volunteer at the Southern Arizona VA Health Care System.</p>



  <p>Veterans and staff gathered to commemorate his historic life and service, but the celebration wasn’t just a birthday party—it was a tribute to the sacrifices made by Horn and his fellow soldiers in their fight for freedom and peace.</p>



  <p>In a brief but touching address, Horn, with his characteristically humble demeanor, reflected on his experiences and expressed his appreciation for the support he has received over the years. “It’s been a joy and true, true joy. And you guys have made a big difference in my life, so I thank you,” he said. “It’s an honor to be remembered and celebrated in such a way.”</p>



  <p>Reflecting on his journey, he added, “Look forward. Don’t look back and say it may not be in your hands. I just hope that all you guys with PTSD [that] you’re able to handle it. I don’t think it disappears. I really don’t. But you get to see the signs, and you learn how to handle them. At least that’s been my experience.”</p>



  <p>It’s never too late to apply for VA health care you’ve earned. If you are a Veteran or know a Veteran who has not yet applied for their VA health care benefits, learn how to <a>get started</a>.</p>

The Tucson VET Center celebrated the 102nd birthday of Harvey Horn, a former World War II Prisoner of War and volunteer at the Southern Arizona VA Health Care System. Veterans and staff gathered not just for a birthday party, but to honor Horn's significant contributions and sacrifices in the fight for freedom and peace. In a heartfelt address, Horn expressed his gratitude for the support he has received throughout his life. He remarked, “It’s been a joy and true, true joy. And you guys have made a big difference in my life, so I thank you.” Horn also shared reflections on his experiences, encouraging others to look forward and manage their challenges. He acknowledged the ongoing struggles with PTSD, stating that while it may not disappear, individuals can learn to recognize and cope with its signs. The event served as a reminder of the importance of mental health support for veterans, encouraging those who have not yet applied for VA health care benefits to do so.
